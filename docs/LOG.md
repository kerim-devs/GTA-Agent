### Ways of control, 12 November afternoon

What a search of the published work gave, read from the papers and repositories and measured by nobody here. NitroGen (NVIDIA, arXiv 2601.02427): about 493 million parameters, one frame of 256 by 256 in, a chunk of 16 gamepad actions out after 16 flow steps; its paper leaves real-time use to future work and gives no time per request and no memory; the server in its repository speaks pickle over ZeroMQ and binds to every interface, the client runs on Windows only, the licence is non-commercial, and whether GTA V was among its games could not be read. Behavioural cloning on Counter-Strike (arXiv 2104.04258) ran at 16 frames a second from the picture alone. Lumine (arXiv 2511.08892) perceives at 5 Hz and acts at 30 Hz with a first chunk after 114 ms, on four server cards, and no weights are out. The robots' answer to the same problem is two clocks: Helix runs a 7B model at 7 to 9 Hz beside an 80 million parameter policy at 200 Hz, GR00T N1 a model at 10 Hz beside a head at 120 Hz, and real-time chunking (arXiv 2506.07339) computes the next chunk while the current one runs. Light detectors are an order faster than ours on paper (YOLO26n 0.84 ms on an RTX 4090 with TensorRT, RF-DETR-N 2.3 ms on a T4); none of these figures is for a Mac. A person's simple visual reaction is 231 ms in a study of 1469 people (Woods 2015); no figure was found for a shooter on a controller. Most benchmarks for game agents pause the game while the model thinks, which hides exactly what is asked here.
