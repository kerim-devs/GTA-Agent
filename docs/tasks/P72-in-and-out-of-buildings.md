# P72: In and out of buildings: not through a door by accident, in on purpose, and out the way it came

## Why

In the night of 13 November a walk began in front of the barber's door in Sandy Shores. Its target lay behind the shop, the planner's first stretch was two degrees off straight ahead, the figure ran in at the door, the shop's welcome took the map away and the walk ended. From inside it took 65 s and three tries to get out, twice by way of the barred window. Kerim watched: it stands in a corner and turns in circles, it could simply use the way it came in by; how did it get in there at all, why does it walk along there; such things must not happen again, and nobody can sit at one barber's for an hour. His rules of that night are in the log ("The barber's door") and in `docs/WALKTHROUGH.md`, line 3: remember the entrance; if nothing helps, run at every face of the room once; once out, three or four steps straight on before a turn.

Much of that is built and has not run at the console (`walkto.py`, `wayround.py`, `ahead.py`, commits `03c95f5` and `e30997a`): the walk asks the local model once a second whether it is in a room, goes back out the way it came, waits a scene out, looks for the way out and goes round along the walls. Four things are missing. All of it leans on the local model, and the gaming PC it runs on is switched off for hours at a time: without the model the walk does not know it is indoors. The walk still starts through a door it stands in front of. Going in on purpose does not exist, and the errands of card P70 need it. And going out exists only as the first part of a walk to somewhere.

Read `PLAN.md`, `docs/tasks/README.md`, the log of 13 November in full, then `pilot/src/pilot/walkto.py`, `wayround.py`, `waymark.py`, `ahead.py`, `foot.py`, `sides.py` and their tests, and `lab/ahead/` (the scripts that measured the model, with `frames.json`, `answers.json` and `indoors.json`: the model's answers on recorded frames, which are your labels while the PC is off). The sessions of the night are under `/Users/kerim/recordings/pilot/`: `20261113-0011` to `-0116` (walks in the open, hold-ups, the porch), `20261113-022635` to `-025632` (the door, and the three walks begun inside the barber's). Each has `video.mp4`, `frames.jsonl`, `play.jsonl` (one row a tick: reading, skill, pad). Kerim's own play of the same night (`/Users/kerim/recordings/kerim/20261113-03*` and `-04*`) has him going into shops and out again with the controller log beside it.

## What to do, in this order

Indoors without a model. The log has two facts to build on: in a room the game draws the minimap far closer (a point carried by the map's own motion ran off by more than a thousand pixels in a minute, against 182 in a walk of 232 m in the open), and the arrow is read in a room as well. Find a reading that says "indoors" from the picture's own numbers, with no model: the map's motion per second against the stick (`foot.map_shift`), the width of what the map draws, the map's look in a room (few roads, the room's outline, the scene showing through), whatever holds. Label frames with the model's recorded answers and with the play logs (the walks begun in the barber's are indoors until the door; Kerim's shop visits by what the frames show), keep a part aside that the reading is not tuned on, and report how often it is right indoors and outdoors and how long after the door it says so. It becomes a function in a module of yours that returns indoors, outdoors or None, and `walkto.py` uses it wherever it asks the model today: the model's answer where one is fresh, this reading where there is none, and where both speak and disagree, the report says which was right more often on the frames and that one wins.

Not through a door by accident. A walk that begins within a few map pixels of a place's symbol, or held against a wall, stands at a building; a walk whose first stretch leads into what the planner cannot see is the barber's door again. Measure on the walks of the night what the picture gave at their start (where the symbol stood, where the building blocks of the map lay where the map draws them, what the model said of "straight ahead" on the recorded frames), and build the start so that such a walk first goes away from the building, along its front, and only then turns to its target: no straight stretch through a place's symbol, and no first step at a wall the model or the map shows. Replay the three starts at the barber's (`20261113-022849`, the walk into the door, and the starts of the loops of 00:54 to 01:16 that began on the shop's symbol) and show which way the walk now sets out.

In on purpose. `walk_to_point` has the argument `enter` (it does nothing yet). With `enter: true` and `exact: true`, once the figure is at the place's symbol or held against its wall, it goes for the door: with the model, by asking for the entrance in the three parts of the picture as `ahead.py` asks for the way out; without it, along the wall with the stick pushed at an angle into it, which slides the figure through any door in it, round the corner where the wall ends, for forty seconds at the most. Inside (the reading above or the model, twice running, or the shop's welcome scene) it lets go and ends done `entered`, and remembers for the session, by the place's name where it has one, on which side of the symbol the door was and the heading it went in by. A walk that cannot find a door ends done `arrived; no door found`, standing off the wall, not failed: the errand goes on without the shop.

Out, as a skill of its own. `leave_building` in `leave.py` (today a stub with the name, arguments and prompt; keep the name and arguments): for a figure indoors it does what the walk does before its first step, in Kerim's order: back out by the remembered heading of the way in where there is one (the camera turned by the arrow, as `WayRound.back_out` does), else the model's look for the way out, else the round along the walls; then a second and a half straight on in the open. It ends done `outdoors`, also when it began outdoors (after two readings a third of a second apart), and fails `no-way-out` or `time-limit` with a neutral pad. Reuse the guard's machinery; do not write a second one beside it.

How long each took. For the report, replay or simulate the three ways out on the recorded frames as far as a replay can (the camera does not turn in a replay; say where that ends the evidence) and give the seconds each way needs by its own constants, so that the reviewer can see that a way out costs at most a few seconds where the entrance is remembered.
