# P62: The reaction bench: several ways of control on the same scenes, timed from the picture to the input

## Why

Kerim's order of 12 November, 15:50, on a question from a programmer friend: a pilot that reads the picture with a model is always late in a fast scene. In a shootout the figure has to protect itself, and one or two seconds until anything happens is too slow. Several ways of controlling the game are to be researched and built, inside one frame for testing and measuring, and run one after another, so that afterwards it is known which of them was technically better.

What exists are pieces, each measured alone. An input shows in the picture after 120 to 156 ms. The minimap reader takes 1 ms, the detector 40 ms and delivers about 15 results a second, the judge answers in 0.25 s and is believed after two answers in a row, the head of rules decides in 0.03 s, the model head in 5.5 to 9 s. The bench of P58 counts whether a capability succeeds and not how fast the pilot reacts. Nobody has timed the way from the frame in which a threat appears to the input that answers it, and nothing compares two ways of control on the same scenes. `fight`, `melee` and `punch` have never run on the console and have no entry in the bench's catalogue.

Read `PLAN.md`, `docs/LOG.md` from "The judge, measured", `docs/tasks/P58-the-bench.md` and `lab/bench/README.md` (the frame this one sits beside), `pilot/src/pilot/contract.py`, `loop.py`, `conductor.py`, `replay.py`, `combat.py`, `judge.py`, `objects.py`, `hud.py`, `head.py` with the rules, and under `/Users/kerim/recordings/derived/kerim/` two or three folders to see what the labels hold (`actions.jsonl` with stretches of `aim` and `fire`, `hud.jsonl` with health, stars and the count of red, `incidents.jsonl`, `labels.json` with the lead of the hand over the picture).

## What to build

A module `pilot/src/pilot/reaction.py`, a module `pilot/src/pilot/approaches.py` and a tool `pilot/tools/reaction.py` beside the other tools, with these parts.

The measure. For one way of control on one scene: the reaction, which is the time from the onset, the capture time of the first frame in which the threat can be read, to the first pad state that answers it; the same with the link's delay added, which is when the picture would show it (0.135 s, the median of the log, as a named constant with its source); the decision time from the capture of a frame to the pad computed from it; the age of the newest frame, detection and judgment at the moment of the answer; the decisions per second; whether it answered at all within three seconds; and every answer given where nothing asked for one. Over a set of scenes: the share answered, the median, the ninth decile and the worst reaction, and the false alarms per calm scene and per minute. A way of control that presses something all the time must not look fast: an answer in the two seconds before the onset voids the scene for it and counts as a false alarm, and the calm scenes count the rest.

The scenes. A scene is a stretch of a recording, named by the recording, the onset and a kind, and never copied: six seconds before the onset and four after it. Three kinds to begin with: an enemy appears (the first red blip, or the first armed person in the open), the figure is hit (health falls), stars light up. For each kind the set of inputs that count as an answer is written down in one place, as data: aiming (L2), firing (R2), cover (R1), or running away with the sprint, and say why each is in the set. Candidates come from the labels: wherever one of the three readings changes and Kerim aims or fires within five seconds. The onset is set from the picture and not from his hand: the reader's first changed frame proposes it, and a sheet of the frames around it, which you look at, confirms or moves it; say for every scene how its onset was fixed and how sure it is, and drop a scene whose onset cannot be told within two frames. The aim is thirty scenes with a threat and thirty calm ones taken from the same recordings (walking and driving with nothing red, no stars, full health); say how many there are if the recordings hold fewer, and what footage is missing. The list lives in `lab/reaction/scenes.json`, and `reaction.py scenes --sheets` rebuilds the sheets.

Kerim as the reference. For every scene with a threat his own reaction is taken from `pad.jsonl`: the onset to his first answering input. His hand and the recorded picture are not on one clock in every recording (`labels.json` gives the lead and whether it was measured or estimated); say how you bring them onto one clock and what error remains. His median and ninth decile are the row every way of control is read against.

The replay. A way of control is handed the frames of a scene at the times they were captured, on the wall clock at the speed of the recording, and is asked for a pad state thirty times a second, as the loop does it live; what it costs to compute is spent for real, on this machine or on the server it calls, so that a slow model is late in the replay as it would be on the console. The picture does not answer to its inputs, which is enough for the first reaction and for nothing after it; the report says so. A second mode runs as fast as it can and adds the measured costs from a table, for the tests and for a quick look, and its numbers are marked as modelled wherever they appear.

The ways of control. Each is one small class in `approaches.py` behind one interface (take a frame with its capture time, give the pad for now, close), registered by name, and adding one is one entry; `lab/reaction/README.md` says how in half a page with a worked example. Build these:

| name | what decides | expected weak point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reflex` | the readers alone, on every tick: a red blip, falling health or stars start `Fight` or the flight at once | false alarms, blind to what the readers do not read |
| `pilot` | the pilot as it plays today: events, the head of rules, the plan, the skill | the windows of acceptance along the chain |
| `judge` | the vision model on the gaming PC, asked one fixed question about every frame it can take, believed at the first answer or at the second (two variants) | a quarter second per answer |
| `head` | the model head, asked with the frame when something changes | seconds |
| `reflex+judge` | `reflex` answers at once with the cheapest protective input, the judge keeps or withdraws it | how long a false alarm is held |
| `policy` | a model on the gaming PC that turns frames into pad states (NitroGen is the first candidate) | not built here: define the request and the reply over HTTP, a stub that answers after a set delay, and leave the server to P63 |

For `judge`, `head` and `reflex+judge` the model is reached over the network and you have none: build them against a small local server of your own that answers after a set delay with a set answer, test them there, and give the exact commands with which the reviewer runs them against `http://192.168.0.248:1234` and the Codex head. The judge's question for this bench is new; write it in the style of `judge.py` with fixed answers, as a constant in `approaches.py`, and do not change `judge.py`.

The ledger and the board. One line per way of control and scene in a file that only grows, by default `lab/reaction/ledger.jsonl`, with the commit of the code, whether the tree was dirty, the mode (real time or modelled), the machine, and the numbers above. `reaction.py run <name>... [--scenes ...] [--repeat N]` appends, `reaction.py board` prints and writes one Markdown and one HTML file without outside assets: per way of control the share answered, median, ninth decile and worst reaction, with and without the link, the false alarms, the decision time and rate, Kerim's row above them, and under the table one timeline per scene with the onset, Kerim's answer and each way's answer on it, so that a look shows who was first. The board states for every number whether it was measured in real time or modelled.

What the console needs later, described and not built: which fields a session's `play.jsonl` already has for timing a reaction live and which are missing, how a try of "a threat appears" would be staged and proved in the bench of P58 (the catalogue entry, the start state, the proof pieces), and how the ways of control would be switched in `pilot play`. The reviewer wires that.

## Judging it

Run `reflex` and `pilot` in real time over every scene and show the board. For three scenes of each kind put the frame of the onset, the frame of Kerim's answer and the frame of each way's answer side by side in the report. Say where a way of control answered before Kerim and where after, and for the slowest three scenes of `pilot` which link of the chain held the time, in milliseconds per link. Tests on small scenes written in the test: the reaction from a known onset and a known answer, an answer before the onset voided, a calm scene with an input counted as a false alarm, a slow way of control that is late by its delay in real time and by the table in the modelled mode, the judge believed at the second answer, the withdrawal in `reflex+judge`, and a new way of control added by one entry.
