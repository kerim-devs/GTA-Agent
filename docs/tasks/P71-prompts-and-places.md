# P71: What the game offers: the help text's button, and using a shop from the inside

## Why

Kerim wants the pilot to do what a player does between missions: get a haircut, go into a clothes shop, change at home. And inside a mission the game itself says, in the help text at the top left, which button does the next thing ("Drücke [button], um ..."). Nothing in the pilot reads that offer today: the text reader gives the words of the help text, but the button is a glyph it does not read, and no skill presses it. In the night of 13 November Kerim was asked to go into shops and use the prompts with the recorder running, so what a player presses at which help text is in his recordings and does not have to be guessed.

Read `PLAN.md`, `docs/tasks/README.md`, `pilot/src/pilot/scene.py` (the text reader and its regions), `flow.py` (the screen reflex and how a skill that opens a menu is spared by it: `opens` and `Flow.expect`), `hud.py` (the screen classes), `legend.py` (how a skill presses a button once: every button is let go on the first tick after its press that read the screen), `contract.py`, `profile.py`, and the log of 13 November in `docs/LOG.md`, "The waypoint that was called set": a press that spans a slow tick becomes two at the console, because the bridge lets a pad go after 250 ms.

## What to do, in this order

What Kerim presses at a help text. For every help text in his recordings that asks for a button (it holds `Drücke`, `Halte` or `Tippe`), find what he pressed while it stood and in the second after it went: which button, how long after the text appeared, pressed or held and for how long. Group the texts by what they ask (the reader's spelling varies; `notes.py` has what was used for objectives). The table in the report gives, for each kind of prompt, the count, the button and how sure it is, and it is kept as data in `pilot/src/pilot/prompts.json` (the wording that identifies the prompt, the button, press or hold and its seconds, how often seen). Where the frames let you, cut the glyph itself out of the picture at the place in the line where the button stands and see whether the glyphs can be told apart by a template (cross, circle, square, triangle, the four directions, L1, R1, L2, R2, L3, R3): if nine in ten of the cut glyphs are named rightly on frames the templates were not made from, the reading of the glyph goes first and the table second; if not, say so and the table stands alone.

`prompts.py`: `offer(text, frame=None)` returns what the help text asks for, or None: the button, press or hold and for how long, the wording it matched, and whether it is known from the recordings or a guess. A prompt that is not in the table and whose glyph is not read is a guess of the game's context button (d-pad right in GTA V, by `profile.py` once you have added it there under a name of its own), and the guess is marked as one. Some prompts are never followed whatever they ask: anything that opens the phone, the internet, a save, the stock market, a purchase of property, GTA Online or the PlayStation's own menus. That list is at the top of the module, each line with the wording that triggers it.

`do_prompt`, the skill: presses what `offer` returns for the help text on the screen, once, as a single press at the console (down on one tick, held until the first tick after it that read the screen and at most 0.2 s, or held for the seconds a hold asks), and ends done `pressed: <button>` when the help text has changed or gone within two seconds of the press, failed `no-effect` when it still stands, failed `no-prompt` when there is no offer, and failed `refused: <why>` for a prompt of the forbidden list. It needs the text of the screen: take it from the frame with `scene.TextReader` in a thread of your own as `legend.py` and `waypoint.py` do, never in the tick.

What a shop looks like from the inside. In the recordings of 13 November find Kerim's visits to the barber's, a clothes shop, Ammu-Nation, a tattoo studio and his safehouse's wardrobe, as far as there are any: from the help text at the chair, the counter or the rack to the moment he walks again. For each: what the screen class reader calls the menu, what the text reader reads of it (title, entries, prices, the footer), which buttons he pressed in what order, how the menu is left, how long it took. This is the measure for the skill and goes into the report as a table, with a sheet of frames for each kind of shop under `lab/prompts/`.

`use_place`, the skill, for a figure that stands inside a shop or at the safehouse's wardrobe. It looks for the offer: if the help text names none, it walks slowly forward and turns a quarter at a time, at most twice round, until one shows (the chair, the counter and the racks each show theirs within a step or two); a shop's welcome with no map on the screen is waited out. It presses the offer's button and waits for the menu. In the menu it reads the entries and their prices, moves down a number of rows drawn at random from the seed it was given (argument `choice`: `random`, or `first` for the first row), goes one level in if the row opens a list, and confirms one entry once, if `spend` is `yes` and the price it read is at most the limit (a class constant, 150 dollars to begin with, with a comment that Kerim sets it); with `spend` `no`, or an entry too dear, it confirms nothing. Then it backs out of every level of the menu with the back button until the game's own screen is there again and the figure can walk, and ends done `used: <prompt>; bought: <entry> for <price>` or `; bought nothing`. It fails `no-prompt`, `menu-unread` (after backing out), `wrong-screen: <name>` or `time-limit`, each with the menu closed and a neutral pad. While its menu is open the flow reflex must leave it alone: set `opens` to the screen classes the reader gives these menus, and if the reader has no class for them or the wrong one, the report says which frames, because `hud.py` is not yours.

Every press in these skills is one press at the console: the lesson of 13 November is in the log, and a second cross in a shop's menu buys twice.
