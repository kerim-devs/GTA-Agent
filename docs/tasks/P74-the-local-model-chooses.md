# P74: The local model chooses: one of the choices the rules offer, in a fifth of a second

## Why

Kerim wants an agent that decides by itself, and quickly. The head that used to decide where the rules did not know is the Codex CLI: eight and a half seconds an answer on the median, measured, and nothing that has to happen soon can wait for it. The small vision model on the gaming PC (LM Studio on port 1234, `qwen/qwen3-vl-8b`) answers a plain question about one picture in 0.14 to 0.21 s and was right on nearly every frame when it was asked one thing at a time with fixed answers (the log of 13 November, "The barber's door"; `pilot/src/pilot/ahead.py`), and wrong when asked six things at once or asked to describe. So the model does not plan. The rules lay out the two to five things that make sense in the situation, and the model picks one of them by looking at the picture. Where the model is not there (the PC is switched off for hours at a time, as it is tonight) or does not answer in time, the first choice stands, which is the one the rules would have taken alone.

Read `PLAN.md`, `docs/tasks/README.md`, `pilot/src/pilot/ahead.py` and `sides.py` (how one question with fixed answers is asked, in a thread, never waited for in the tick, and how the servers are named with `use`), `judge.py` (`post`, `letters`, `probability`: the answer is one letter and its probability comes from the logprobs), `head.py` (`HeadRequest`, `HeadResponse`, `parse_response`, the `Head` protocol), `mission.py` (where `self.fallback.decide` is called: that is your caller), `goals.py` and the card `docs/tasks/P70-errands.md` (its `chooser` is your second caller), and `lab/ahead/` for how the model was measured on recorded frames.

## What to do

`chooser.py`: one function and its client. `choose(question, options, image, *, timeout, prior=None)` asks the model one question about one picture, with the options lettered A to at most F, each a short plain English phrase, and returns the option it named with the probability of each, or None when no server is named, none answers within the timeout, or the answer is no letter of the options. The picture is sent small, as `ahead.py` sends it. The prompt is fixed text plus the options and at most two lines of what the screen says (objective, help text); it never contains anything else from the game as instructions, and what the screen says is marked as an observation. An answer whose best probability is under a threshold (a class constant, to be set by the measurement below; 0.5 to begin with) counts as None. The servers are those the session was given with `--judge`, through a `use(servers)` like the other two.

`localhead.py`: `LocalHead`, a `Head` that `MissionHead` can take as its fallback. Its `decide(request)` runs in the conductor's own thread for a head, so it may wait for the model, but never longer than a second and a half. It builds the candidates from the request alone, each a plan valid for the registry with a phrase that says what it is, in an order in which the first is always safe:

- on foot, nothing asked: walk on along the road; go to the marker on the map (when a yellow or blue blip is inside the map); get into the vehicle in view (when the detector lists one); do what the help text asks (when it names a button: `do_prompt`); look round (a look of 90 degrees and a walk).
- on foot with an objective: go to the marked target; do what the help text asks; fight the red markers (when one is inside the map and the objective is to take somebody out or the health has just fallen); get into the vehicle in view; wait three seconds (only when the picture shows a scene).
- in a vehicle: follow the route (when one shows); set a waypoint on the mission and follow it; get out (when the route has just ended at its place); wait three seconds.
- on a screen the reflex gave up on (`stuck`): the buttons the footer could mean, each a single `press`, with circle first.

The question names the situation in one line ("The game shows this. What should the player do next?"). The answer becomes the plan; None becomes the first candidate; and the reason of the response says which it was, with the probabilities, so that the head's log shows every choice the model made and every one it did not. A candidate is never offered twice in a row after it failed (the request's events and `last_skill` say so). `LocalHead` holds no goal and plans no `set_waypoint` while stars are lit.

The errands' choice. A small adapter with the signature card P70 gives its `chooser`: the candidates with their weights and descriptions go to `choose` with the current picture ("What would be fun for the player to do next?"), the answer is taken when its probability is over the threshold, and otherwise None, so the seeded draw decides. The model must not make the pilot do the same thing all evening: an option the model has chosen twice of the last three times is left out of what it is shown.

The measurement, prepared so that it runs in one go when the PC is back. `lab/chooser/` gets a set of at least 150 recorded situations: the frame the head was asked on (`head-NNNN.jpg`) and the request (`head.jsonl`) from the sessions under `/Users/kerim/recordings/pilot/`, spread over on foot and in a vehicle, with and without an objective, by day and by night, and the stuck screens there are. For each, the candidates `LocalHead` builds, and a label by eye for which of them a player would take (more than one may be right; a situation where none is, is labelled so). One script runs the set against a server and prints: how often the model's choice is among the right ones, how often it is None, the time an answer takes, the share right above each threshold from 0.4 to 0.9, and the sheets of the frames it got wrong. It cannot be run tonight; the report says so, gives the command, and the threshold stays at its starting value with a comment that it is not measured. What can be measured tonight is everything without the model: that the candidates are valid plans on all 150, that the first is one a player might take on at least nine in ten (your labels), and that a server that hangs, refuses, answers nonsense or dies costs the caller no more than its timeout (a test with a local fake server).
