# P78: Driving at a marker: after a car that flees, behind one to follow, and to a place the game draws no line to

## Why

`drive_route` follows the line the game draws on the minimap, yellow for a mission's destination and purple for a waypoint. Much of a mission has no line. "Folge", "Verfolge", "Hol ihn ein", "Bleib an ihm dran": the car to follow or to catch is a marker on the minimap, blue or red, that moves, and the game draws nothing between it and the player. The pilot has no skill for that: in a vehicle with an objective and no route the rules set a waypoint on the map, which leads somewhere else while the mission fails. In Kerim's recordings such stretches are among the ones he drives hardest (`20261110-093238`, the chase on the motorbike, 150 to 210 s; the missions of 11 and 12 November under `/Users/kerim/recordings/derived/kerim/<name>/missions.json`, which lists every objective with its class and what he did).

`get_away` (card P68) already drives without a line: it takes a heading, keeps to a road that leads within 45 degrees of it where the minimap shows one (`terrain.py`), goes across country where none does, steers round what the detector sees, stays out of water and frees a held car with the driver's own recoveries. With the heading taken from a marker instead of away from the police, that is most of this skill.

Read `PLAN.md`, `docs/tasks/README.md`, `docs/reports/P68-getting-away.md`, then `pilot/src/pilot/getaway.py`, `terrain.py`, `drive.py`, `avoid.py`, `minimap.py` (what a `Blip` is and how the reader finds one: colour, position relative to the arrow in map pixels, the border flag for a marker pinned to the map's rim), `contract.py`, `sim.py` and `pilot/tests/test_getaway.py` (its simulated worlds, which you extend).

Card P73 is changing `drive.py`, `avoid.py`, `getaway.py` and `terrain.py` in another worktree tonight. Use them as they are in yours, through what they offer to a caller (`Driver` and its `step`, `Avoider`, `terrain.read` and its fields, the helpers of `getaway.py` you can import), and change none of them: where you need something from one of them that it does not offer, write it in your own module and say so in the report, so that the reviewer can join the two.

## What to do, in this order

What Kerim does. From `missions.json` and `actions.jsonl` of his recordings find the stretches in a vehicle with an objective on the screen and no route on the minimap, with a blue, red or yellow marker on it. For each: the objective's words, the marker's colour, how long it lasted, how it ended (the objective changed, the mission failed and why if the banner says so, a cutscene), how far from the arrow the marker was over the stretch (in map pixels: near, far, pinned to the rim), how he steered against the marker's bearing (does he head straight at it or keep to the road), and his speed against the pedal. A table in the report. This says what the skill has to do and how close it has to stay: a car to follow must not be passed or rammed, a car to catch must be.

`drive_to_blip`, the skill, as one class in a module of its own under the rules for a new skill. Arguments: `color` (`blue`, `red`, `yellow`), `mode` (`reach`: drive to it and end when it is under the arrow; `follow`: stay behind it at a distance and never end by reaching it; `catch`: stay on it as closely as the car allows), and `duration`. The heading is the marker's bearing from the arrow, smoothed against the reader's jitter and held for up to two seconds over ticks on which the marker is not read (an icon over it, a damaged picture), after which the pedal comes up. The road is kept where one leads within 45 degrees of the heading and left where none does, as `get_away` does it; water is never entered; what the detector sees ahead is steered round; a held car is freed by the driver's recoveries. A marker pinned to the rim is far away: full speed along the road. A marker inside the map is near: in `follow` the speed is held so that the marker stays at the distance Kerim keeps (from the table), in `reach` the car slows for the last stretch and the skill ends done `reached` with the marker under the arrow for half a second, in `catch` the pedal stays down. A marker behind the car for more than a second is turned round to, with the driver's own turn. It ends failed `blip-gone` when no marker of the colour has been read for five seconds, `vehicle-held` after the recoveries, `on-foot` when the figure is no longer seated, `water`, `screen-<name>`, and `time-limit`, each with a neutral pad, and it presses no button.

In the simulation. Extend the worlds of `test_getaway.py` with a second car that drives along the roads as a marker on the drawn minimap: it flees at a speed the player's car can match, it drives at traffic speed, it turns at junctions, it stops. Measure over a minute each and on every car profile of the simulator: in `follow`, the share of time within the wanted distance and whether the marker was ever lost or passed; in `catch`, the time to reach it and the share of time within two car lengths; in `reach` to a standing marker 300 m off across two junctions, the time to arrive and where the car stands at the end.

On recorded frames. On the stretches of the table, replay the skill tick by tick over the recorded pictures (the minimap reader on each frame's crop, as the conductor would hand it over) and set its heading beside Kerim's stick as P68 did for the escapes (`lab/getaway/scripts/beside.py`): how often it steers the way he does when he steers clearly. A replay does not answer the skill's pad, so this says whether the heading is sensible and nothing about the driving.
