# P105: Metres on the map

## Why

The speed law of P98 and P102 turns pixels of the route on the minimap into metres with one number, `Driver._map_scale(speed)` in `drive.py`: 0.7 m/px below 6 m/s, 1.0 below 12, 2.1 below 18, 3.6 below 24, 3.7 above, and never under `map_scale_min`, which is 1.4 with the bold style, so that the bold driver plans with 1.4 below 12 m/s, 2.1 below 18 and 3.6 or 3.7 above. Every distance the law plans with comes from it: how far the next bend is, how much road the map shows (`visibility`), where a remembered corner ends. The numbers were set from what the map was assumed to do, zoom out with the speed, and from a low-speed median; they were never measured against the road.

In town the bold driver makes 7.5 to 9 metres of route a second over a whole drive (the arrivals of 14 November from 02:44 on: `/Users/kerim/recordings/pilot/20261114-024431`, `-024817`, `-025007`, `-025345`, `-025645`), where Kerim's straights are at 18 to 22 and his turns at 14 to 21. The drive's time goes to the speed law's limits: a quarter to a half under `curve-exit` at 6 to 8 m/s, a fifth under `curve` at 11 to 12, under `straight` hardly any. If the law believes a junction to be 50 m away that is 100 m away, it slows half a block early; if it believes one to be 180 m away that is 90 m away, it arrives too fast.

A first look says both happen. Every tick of `play.jsonl` has the reading with `path` (the route in crop pixels from the arrow, x right, y ahead), `gps_distance` (the printed metres of route to the destination) and `gps_speed`. I took the first bend of 60 degrees or more along the path, followed it from tick to tick until it was within 30 px, and set the path length to it in pixels against the metres of route the car then still drove to that point (`lab/drive/p105-first-look.py`, over fifteen drives of the night; only 18 corners were followed cleanly, so these are a hint and not a measurement). Metres per pixel of path, tenth percentile to median: below 8 m/s 1.2 to 2.8 where the bold law plans with 1.4; at 8 to 16 m/s 1.2 to 3.0 where it plans with 1.4 to 2.1; above 16 m/s 1.6 to 3.1 where it plans with 2.1 to 3.7. So at the median a bend in town is up to twice as far as the law believes, and above 18 m/s it is nearer than the law believes, by the low percentile by half. The map is drawn tilted while driving, so a pixel far up the map should be more metres than one at the arrow, and one number for all of them cannot be right at both ends.
