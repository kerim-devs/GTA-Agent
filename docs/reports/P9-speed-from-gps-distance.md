## Review

Checked on 10 November. The default gate passes in 17 s with the native recognition running. Six breaks each fail a test: no slowing on arrival, braking without knowing that the car rolls, no extension of the route under the arrow, a jump of the distance counted as speed, a speed that never expires, and any number accepted as metres.

Outside the sandbox the recognition works through this code: the worker's probe reads five of its ten frames in 13 ms at the median. It also leaked. Every call kept its enlarged patch, 170 KB, because the pixels were handed to Core Graphics as Python bytes and no autorelease pool was drained: 168 MB over 1000 calls, about 2.4 GB an hour at four readings a second. The image now goes over as `NSData` inside a pool, growth is 0.6 MB over 300 calls, and a test on a real crop pins both the reading (326 m) and the memory. The same pattern is in `scene.py` with a full frame per call and is fixed separately.

A full replay of the long recording through this pipeline gives a speed on 43% of the ticks of the driving stretch with a median of 21.8 m/s, against the 20.5 m/s of the whole stretch, and no speed anywhere outside a route. The accuracy target is missed as reported, and shorter windows are worse (55% at 2 s, 45% at 1.5 s), so it is noise and not only lag. The frame times of the Wi-Fi recording jitter by tenths of a second, which at 20 m/s is several metres per reading; whether the estimate is good enough has to be decided on a wired recording, where a reading rate of ten a second is affordable.

The aiming point was drawn on twelve real frames of the stretch. On the eleven with a normal route it sits on the route line just ahead of the arrow or at the corner of the junction ahead. On the last one, 63 m before the destination with three path points, the extended line points away from the route and the target lands beside it. Short paths at the end of a route need a look once the car drives.
