# P44 On foot and not getting anywhere

On the evening of 11 November the pilot was started on the pier, on foot, with no car in sight (session `/Users/kerim/recordings/pilot/20261111-204712`). For three minutes the rules planned look, five seconds of walking straight ahead, look, five seconds of walking, and `walk_to` whenever a blue blip showed, which failed with `blip-gone` after two or three seconds. Kerim watched the figure walk against the railing and the side of the stairs the whole time. This card finds out from the recording what happened, and builds walking that notices it gets nowhere, turns away from what the judge calls blocked, and heads for a road when nothing is marked.

## What the session shows

How standing still is told on foot. The minimap on foot turns with the camera about the player arrow and slides under it as the figure walks. The pipeline's `moving` measures the size of the map's motion, so a camera turn counts as motion just as a step does: on the pier it called the map moving on 237 ticks on which the ground under the arrow had not moved. The measure used here is new, `pilot.foot.map_shift`: features on the map are followed over half a second, a turn about the arrow and a shift are fitted together, and the shift alone is the ground passing under the figure. On this map a walking figure moves the ground 0.7 to 1.0 crop pixels in half a second, a sprinting one 2 to 3.5, and a figure pressed against the railing less than 0.15 (median 0.0 to 0.1 per second of it). Where the map cannot be followed, open water being one even white, the pipeline's stationary band (`moving` False) is taken instead, because a camera turn cannot fake it. The picture outside the HUD was measured as well and is not used: the mean change over half a second is 2.0 on still ticks and 10.7 on walking ones on the pier, but on the walking session ticks of a moving figure go as low as 1, and water and passers-by move on their own. The table per tick is `lab/foot/stuck-session.jsonl`, written by `lab/foot/measure.py`; `lab/foot/session.py` reads it.

In numbers. Of the 180 seconds (4560 ticks with a fresh frame), the left stick was pushed at least halfway for 144 s. On 88 s of those the ground under the arrow did not move, on 33 s it did, and on 23 s it was in between or could not be read. The stuck stretches begin at 17 s and do not end: under the bleachers at the side of the stairs (18 to 33 s, 104 to 122 s), and at the railing at the end of the pier (52 to 56 s, 60 to 73 s, 88 to 96 s, 143 to 146 s, 162 to 168 s, 172 to 180 s). The walks that got anywhere are the first seconds after each look, until the next railing. The sheet `lab/foot/pier-stuck.jpg` shows the figure at every moment the replay below calls it stuck.

What the judge said about the path. 608 judgments arrived in the three minutes, every one of them "foot" and every one of them "free". On the seconds the figure stood against the railing the probability the model gave "blocked" was at most 0.02, median 0.0001; on the other seconds at most 0.14. The judge's answer about the path would not have helped here at all, and as the question is asked today it cannot be relied on to notice a railing. The question is in `judge.py`, which this card does not touch.

The blue blip and why `walk_to` lost it. The reader saw a blue blip on 1008 of the 4560 ticks. It is no disc: it is the light-blue question mark of a stranger mission far away, pinned to the rim of the minimap, which slides along the rim as the camera turns. It sat on the left rim (325 ticks, flagged as border), on the right rim, and on the bottom rim just above the health bar (about 330 ticks at y = -22 to -30, not flagged as border, so `walk_to` took it for a target 22 pixels behind the figure and steered the stick backwards, (-0.12, 0.84), towards the railing). `walk_to` ran six times, for 2.1 to 3.9 s each, pushed the stick on at most 35 ticks of a run, and ended `blip-gone` every time: as soon as the figure began to turn, the reader stopped finding the icon although it was still on the map (`lab/foot/pier-walk-to.jpg`, 18.6 to 19.5 s and 56.4 to 57.6 s), and two seconds without it end the skill. The rule `foot_target` therefore walked towards a mission icon that is no target nearby and lost it within a turn, six times.

## What was built

A new module `pilot/src/pilot/foot.py` with three parts, and changes in `skills.py` and `mission.py` that call them.

Noticing and getting free is `FootGuard`, which sits between a walking skill and the pad. The skill says where it wants the left stick; the guard passes it on and watches whether the figure gets anywhere. It counts a stick at least halfway out as walking. Each tick gives evidence: still (shift under 0.3 pixels in half a second, or without a followable map the stationary band), going (shift of 0.5 or more), or nothing. Stuck needs a push that has lasted two seconds and, of the ticks of the last 1.8 seconds, at least six in ten saying still and hardly any saying going; so one odd tick decides nothing, and a damaged tick is stood out with a released left stick and adds nothing. Stuck walks a stretch of 1.6 s in a direction turned away from the wanted one: 70 degrees to the right, then 70 to the left, then 120 to the right, then 120 to the left, and after each stretch the wanted direction is tried again. A way round that itself stands for 1.2 s counts as the next failure at once. Three seconds of a push in which the ground moves clear the count. After four failures in a row the guard reports `stuck` with a released stick, and the skill ends `failed` with that reason; `walk_to` reports it as `blocked`, the word the head already knows from it. Off foot the guard passes the stick through and reads nothing. Sprint is held only on the wanted direction, not on a way round.

The judge's word is in the same guard. When the judge has answered "blocked" in at least three judgments in a row whose frames span 0.9 s, the newest of them less than 1.5 s old, the guard releases the left stick and turns the camera with a right stick of 0.4, which the measured rate turns at 68 degrees a second, until a judgment taken after the turn began says "free" or 100 degrees are reached; then it walks 1.5 s straight into the new view and goes back to the wanted direction. The camera is turned and not the stick, because the judge answers for the view ahead, and a view that does not turn would keep its answer. One blocked judgment, a "cannot tell" in the run, or judgments that are too old turn nothing; successive turns alternate sides; after two turns in a row that never heard "free" the judge is left to the stuck check until the figure gets somewhere. Without a judge none of this runs and the stuck check works alone.

Where to go is `WalkRoad`, a new skill `walk_road`. A road on the minimap is a white band about ten pixels wide; the open water around the pier is white too, but as a sheet, so an opening wider than any road keeps the sheet and leaves the bands. The skill finds the nearest road pixel and walks towards it with the left stick: the map's up is where the camera looks, so a bearing on the map is a direction of the stick and the camera need not turn. On the road it walks along it, the way nearer to where the figure faces, and keeps that way. It is guarded like `walk`. It fails `no-road` after a second of a readable map without a road, `map-unreadable` after two seconds of a map it cannot read, and `no-frame` without a picture; at its time limit it ends `done`. On the walking session `lab/foot/roads.py` finds a road on all 31 frames it sampled, every eight seconds, at a median of 30 pixels (`lab/foot/roads-walking.jpg`); on the pier on 1 of 31, a white strip at the lower edge at 174 s, and none otherwise (`lab/foot/roads-pier.jpg`).

Why both a change and a new skill. Getting nowhere is a fault of walking wherever it happens, so it belongs in `walk` and `walk_to` themselves and not in a skill the head has to remember to choose; `walk_to`'s old sidestep of half a second after 0.8 s of a stationary map is replaced by the guard. Heading for a road is a decision about where to go, which the rules make, so it is a skill of its own that the rules can name and that fails with a reason when there is nothing to head for.

Where the rules call it. `foot_explore`, on foot with nothing marked, no objective and no car, now plans `walk_road` for 15 s instead of the look and walk. When `walk_road` fails `no-road`, a new rule `foot_explore_roadless` plans the old look, walk, look, walk, whose walks are now guarded, and the next plan tries the road again. When `walk` or `walk_road` fails `stuck`, a new rule `foot_stuck` plans a look of 180 degrees over 1.2 s, which asks for full stick and so turns about 175 degrees at the measured 146 degrees a second, and then `walk_road`. Every other decision of the rules is as it was, `walk_to` failing `blocked` included. `head_prompt.txt` names `walk_road` where it told the model to walk towards a road, and the registry gives the model its description.

## Replay

The recorded pixels cannot answer new commands, so the replay shows when the guard would have known and what it would have sent, and nothing about what the figure would then have done. `lab/foot/replay.py` gives each recorded `walk` and `walk_to` a fresh guard, as it would get live, tells it the stick that skill sent, and feeds it the real frames, the readings as recorded, the pipeline's map motion recomputed from the video and the judge's answers at the times they arrived. Up to its first decision what was sent is what the guard would have sent, so that decision is a fair one; what follows it rests on frames that never saw the guard's command.

On the pier (`lab/foot/replay-pier.json`) the session had 34 walking runs. The guard called stuck on 19 of them, the first time 2.0 to 2.4 s into the walk on 13 of them and 2.6 to 4.9 s on the others, at 24.9, 30.3, 39.0, 53.7, 64.1, 71.0, 76.5, 88.0, 93.7, 104.9, 112.6, 120.1, 127.9, 131.7, 143.4, 160.7, 166.2, 172.2 and 178.1 s. Each time the session sent (0, -1), straight ahead, and the guard would have sent (0.94, -0.34), 70 degrees to the right. On the 15 runs it did not call, the walk either moved or ended before two seconds of standing were up: the first stuck stretch at 17.4 s was cut off by the end of the five-second walk at 18.6 s. Within the same runs the replay then lists up to two further ways round, 1.2 s apart, because the recorded figure did not follow; none reached the fourth, since the rules' walks last five seconds. No judge turn, because the judge never said blocked.

On the walking session `/Users/kerim/recordings/pilot/20261110-231512` (`lab/foot/replay-walking.json`), 240 s with 26 walking runs and 3566 walking ticks along roads and over grass, the guard decided nothing: no false alarm. With a window of 1.2 s instead of 1.8 s it had called one, at 122.6 s, and the frames show why: a car had knocked the figure over and it lay on the road for two seconds with the stick pushed (`lab/foot/walking-session.jpg`, 120.5 to 122.5 s). That was a real standstill; the window was lengthened to match the card's two seconds, and it is now too short to be called.

## Tests

`pilot/tests/test_foot.py` pins the behaviour. On recorded crops, the ground at the railing does not move and on a walk it does, and a camera turn of 6 degrees about the arrow, which the plain map motion calls moving, is not a step. Stuck is found between 1.95 and 2.3 s of a push on a stationary map, never while the map moves or cannot be read, never on one still tick a second, never on damaged ticks, which are stood out with a released stick, and never on a push shorter than two seconds. The ways round go 70 right, 70 left, 120 right, 120 left, end in `stuck` with a released stick within the bound, return to the wanted direction after a stretch, and progress clears the count. A judge that says blocked for a second turns the camera with a released left stick and stops at the bound or on a fresh free; successive turns alternate; a single blocked, a stale run and a run broken by "cannot tell" turn nothing; two unanswered turns end the judge's part. `walk` fails `stuck` and `walk_to` `blocked`, both with the neutral pad, sprint is not held on a way round, and the pad is neutral at a time limit, a stop and a replacement in the middle of a way round. `walk_road` walks towards the road it finds, ends neutral at its limit, fails `no-road` over the pier's water and `no-frame` without a picture. In `test_mission.py`, unmarked on foot plans `walk_road`, `no-road` gives the old look and walk, `stuck` turns about, and a `blocked` `walk_to` keeps its old answer. The old test that `walk_to` sidesteps at 0.65 now asserts the guard's alternation. Each of seven deliberate breaks of `foot.py` (a single judgment let through, the lapse lengthened, the sides not alternating, a damaged tick passed on, no share of still ticks required, an unknown map taken as still, a push of half a second enough) fails at least one test.

The full gate, `env -u PS5_HOST .venv/bin/python tools/gate.py --full` from `pilot/`, passed: 2736 passed, 1 skipped, 236 expected failures in 596 s.

## What only the console can show

In the order it should be looked at. First, whether a way round of 70 or 120 degrees on the left stick gets the figure off a railing or a wall at all: the figure turns in an arc, the camera follows it by itself, and 1.6 s may be too short or too long a stretch; the replay cannot show where the figure would have gone. Second, whether the thresholds hold live: the half-pixel shift was measured on two sessions of the far camera on foot, and the map zooms out while sprinting. Third, `walk_road` on a real street: the map turns under the camera while the figure walks, the way along the road is kept against the last one and not against the arrow, and whether it holds through a crossing is not known. Fourth, the judge's turn: in 608 judgments on the pier the judge never said blocked, so whether it ever does, and whether a turn at 68 degrees a second stops near free, can only be seen live. Fifth, `foot_stuck`'s look of 180 degrees, which rests on the measured full-stick rate.

## What other files would need

The blip reader in `minimap.py` takes the stranger mission's question-mark icon on the rim for a blue blip, finds it on only some frames, and does not flag it as border on the bottom rim; `walk_to` then walks backwards towards it. A rim icon should either be flagged as border on every rim or not be a blip. The rule `foot_target` in `mission.py` sends the figure after any blue blip; this card left it as it was, as asked. The judge's question about the path in `judge.py` never said blocked against a railing; it is worth asking it as the figure walking into something rather than the way being free. `play.jsonl` does not carry the pipeline's `moving` nor the guard's state; `conductor.py` could log `Skill.guard.state` beside the skill so that a live session shows when the guard turned away. The conductor's carry between plans pushes (0, -0.45), below the guard's threshold, and is not guarded.
