The head accepts `walk_to` with required `color` in `yellow`, `blue`, `red`, optional `duration` from 0.1 to 10 seconds, and optional boolean `sprint`, default false. `face` accepts the same required color and optional duration from 0.1 to five seconds. Invalid parameters are rejected before replacement of an active skill. Both choose the nearest matching marker and require a readable flat map. Missing, corrupt or vehicle-mode readings immediately produce a neutral pad; two seconds without a usable target fail. Every result uses the full pad contract, bounded sticks and existing release/replacement behavior.

`walk_to` scales left-stick strength with distance and finishes within four map pixels only for an unpinned marker. A border marker provides direction but cannot prove arrival. Sustained blockage triggers alternating half-second sidesteps, with at most three recovery attempts before failure; the overall duration always bounds the run. Sprint pulses are emitted only when explicitly requested and still far from the target. `face` uses a bounded proportional right-stick turn, a ten-degree observation tolerance and a short neutral settling interval. A near-180-degree sign latch prevents noise from repeatedly changing turn direction. Undefined bearing and timeouts fail. The prompt explains these limits, distinguishes color from object identity and map arrival from mission completion, and states that real-game gains are uncalibrated.

Quantization to an older simulation frame can make effective delay about 0.167 seconds. Free walking reaches the target in 20/20 runs, taking at most 3.73 seconds. Corner scenes produce eight arrivals and 12 bounded failures; a sealed obstacle produces 20 bounded failures. No walk exceeds ten seconds. All 120 face runs across starting angles of -179, -90, -20, 20, 90 and 179 degrees finish within 4.5 seconds, with zero turn-direction reversals and true bearing error at most 11 degrees under noisy observations. Two permanent-missing-target runs fail after two seconds. The corner result satisfies bounded failure, not a claim of reliable obstacle navigation.

## Review

Checked on 10 November. The worker was cut off by an application restart while its full gate was still running, so the number missing above comes from me: the full gate passes with 1537 tests and 236 expected failures in 520 s. The reader's numbers on the labelled splits are unchanged, 95.8 and 93.8 per cent for the map class, every route found and none invented, arrow median 2.2 and 1.9 degrees, 0.9 ms per crop on average.

Sixteen breaks were tried. Eleven failed a test straight away. Five did not: finishing `face` on the first aligned frame, flipping the turn direction for a blip straight behind, sprinting without being asked, reporting blips on a damaged frame, and arriving on a pinned marker. The first four are pinned by tests now. The last cannot happen with real readings, because the border of the map is at least 22 pixels from the arrow and arrival needs four.

The labelled set was also the tuning set, so I looked at sixteen crops outside it on which the reader reports something, picked with a fixed seed. Of twenty reported blips one is the yellow end of a drawn route on a vehicle map (crop 973), which the card ruled out, and one red mark in a map corner (crop 2043) I cannot judge; the rest look like real discs. That is good enough to start with, and the wrong one sits on a vehicle map, where neither skill acts.

Three things the report does not say. The reader reports no blips on a damaged frame, and on the Wi-Fi recording 801 of the 2768 crops are damaged, 429 of which carry what look like real blips. Both skills let go on such frames, so over Wi-Fi they would stutter and give up; over the cable the live self-test saw no damaged frame in 533, and that is where they belong. Second, the stuck detection leans on the map-motion flag, which was tuned for driving. Replaying the first 235 s of the long recording, the flag never says stopped while Kerim pushes the stick (681 on-foot frames), so walking is not mistaken for a block, but while he stands it says stopped on only 133 of 1336 frames. A real block will therefore often run to the time limit instead of triggering a sidestep. Third, `face` commands 0.10 to 0.20 on the right stick when it is 10 to 20 degrees off, which may lie inside the game's dead zone; the first live trial has to show whether it needs a floor.

The simulation traces and the source-context sheets, 18 MB together, are not committed; `tools/sim_blip_skills.py` and `tools/inspect_blip_frames.py` rebuild them. The overlay names the two skills in German.
