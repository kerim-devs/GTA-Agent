# P56: the view the pilot needs

The pilot reads the camera's view from the figure's box, and a session begins by setting the far view before it levels the camera: a far figure that the bands name clearly is done at once, otherwise the skill steps through the four views and takes the one with the smallest whole figure. The reading is `read_view` in `pilot/src/pilot/camera.py`, the skill is `set_far_view` in the same file, the opening is `opening()` in `pilot/src/pilot/cli.py`. There was no console, daemon, `ps5rp` command or recording write; the recordings were read, and the detector was run natively on frames of them and of `lab/camera/views/`.

The object reader now hands the conductor the person it cut out as the figure and the vehicle it cut out beside it (`split_player`, `Detector.detect_split`, `Reader.figure`, `Reader.figure_vehicle` in `objects.py`; `Observation.figure` and `figure_vehicle` in `contract.py`; delivered in `run_play`). They carry the capture time of the detections they come from, and the filtered list the avoider and the entry skill use is unchanged. The reading takes the figure's height and where its feet stand, as fractions of the frame: far 0.23 to 0.365 high with feet at 0.66 to 0.87, middle 0.385 to 0.475 and 0.82 to 0.975, near 0.52 to 0.65 and 0.95 or lower. The gaps are left unknown on purpose. The camera's pitch moves them: the far figure is 0.25 to 0.33 high in the game's own camera and 0.34 to 0.355 in the camera that `level_camera` leaves, which is the one a session begins in after the first. A figure that is a rider (a vehicle under its feet and across its body; a motorcycle always, a car or truck only when not wider than 2.2 times the figure), that shares its place with a second person, that stands off the middle of the frame or that the detector is unsure of (under 0.8) is not read. A view is named only once the readings of half a second, from three detections, agree and none of the last second says another. The bands carry the fast path only, because fourteen tries on the console on 12 November reached the far view four times with them alone (below).

On the 140 frames of `lab/camera/sample/` (90 from ten of Kerim's recordings and 50 from five sessions of the pilot's own, labelled by eye and by the presses of `play.jsonl`, with the contact sheets and the raw detections) and the eight pictures, 92 frames are far, middle or near. 89 are named right and none wrong. The other three are two where an onlooker or a larger person at the edge stands in the figure's place and the picture in which a quad hides the figure. With the bands fitted on the other recordings and read on the one left out, 88 of 92 are named right and none wrong. All 25 first-person frames read as no figure, and the four motorcycle frames are not read. The bands were fitted on this sample and tested on it, so the leave-one-recording-out figure is the honest one. `lab/camera/evaluate.py` prints the table and refits.

The first five tries on the console, in `lab/camera/sessions/`, showed what the first bands missed: in two of them the far view stood for six seconds and was never named, because its figure was 0.346 to 0.355 high and the far band ended at 0.345 (36 of 46 and 46 of 49 readings refused for that alone). The bands were refitted, and the nine tries after them showed that no band holds. In `20261112-143356`, `-143406` and `-143436` the figure stood on a pavement in daylight in the near view for ten seconds and the skill named nothing and pressed nothing: its box was 0.66 to 0.74 of the frame high with the feet at 0.99, and the near band ends at 0.65, so 41 of 41, 36 of 39 and 31 of 41 of the readings of the first five seconds were between the bands (three at 0.644 in `-143406` fell inside, and never for half a second). In `-143436` a second person on the pavement made the other 10, from 3.85 s, unreadable as well. The detector's confidence was 0.95 throughout, so it was not refused for that and the confidence floor stays at 0.8. The other tries went: `-143047` and `20261112-141836` far named in 0.59 s and 0.67 s and done with no press; `-143057` not on foot (a highway at night after leaving a buggy); `-143128` the figure had just been run over and the camera looked at the ground; `-143232` just respawned at the hospital, two of its five presses showed no change; `-143302` the box was wider than tall for several seconds after the first press (a figure lying down); `-142428` began in the buggy. The stored detections of these sixteen sessions, with the presses and results, are in `lab/camera/sessions/`; `lab/camera/survey.py` prints what the first two seconds name and what every stretch after a recorded press measures.

The skill has two parts. The fast path: a far view that the bands name clearly within the first two seconds on foot is done with no press (`far; presses=0`). The survey: otherwise it presses, lets the picture settle for 1.5 s, and measures the figure for a second (the median height of its box over the readable detections of the window, whether the lower edge cuts it, or no figure at all), four times, which is one round of the views and ends where it began. The far view is the one whose whole figure is smallest, at most 0.85 of the next larger one; first person is the one without a figure and must come right after it in the round when only one view shows none. Cut figures are never the far one. It then presses on to the far view (zero to three presses), measures after every press and does not count them, so that a press that was lost costs one more and nothing else (two equal neighbours in the round are one view seen twice, and the round goes on until it has four), and ends `done` when the figure there is within 15 percent of the one surveyed. A round in which no view shows a figure, or two views cannot be told apart, or a window cannot be read, fails with the measurements in the reason (`views-alike; last=far; presses=5; survey=0.30/none/0.58cut/0.30`). A window whose figure cannot be used (seen from the front, a second person by it) gets one short step forward of 0.4 s and is measured again. It presses only on the game's screen, only after the mode has read foot for a full second, never with a rider's vehicle under the figure and never on a detection older than 0.6 s; every end is a neutral pad. The hard limit is 50 s (10 to 60 as an argument): a survey from first person is seven presses of 2.8 s and two seconds before them, about 22 s, a camera that shows the figure from the front after every press adds a step and a second to each, about 38 s, and each lost press 3 to 5 s. It cannot be cut short, so the head's first plan waits behind it as it waits behind `level_camera`.

The survey was judged on the model in the tests (`Game` in `tests/test_camera.py`), on 600 runs of it with the detector's noise (a miss in 4 percent of the readings, a box 0.6 to 1.5 times too tall in 3 percent, 4 percent jitter), presses lost at 12 percent each, the camera in front of the figure after a press in one run of three, and four cameras whose bands disagree (the one the bands were fitted on, one looking up, a low one, a high one): 593 ended done and every one of them in the far view, none wrongly; 7 ended failed (six round with three or more lost presses, one lost on the way), none by the limit. On the recorded pictures `lab/camera/replay.py` lets the skill step through the stretches the recorded presses left, where those are four different views: in `20261112-141056` (the idle camera first) it ends `far; presses=4; survey=none/0.57cut/0.49/0.34` at 13.9 s, in `20261112-141430` (first person first) `far; presses=7; survey=0.56cut/0.45/0.35/none` at 20.9 s. The other fourteen sessions show no round of four different views and are not replayed; what their first two seconds and stretches measure is in the tests: far is named only in the two sessions that began in it, and the three pavement stretches measure as a cut figure 0.66 to 0.74 high.

The runner takes the opening skills as a group (`SkillRunner.replace(..., opening=True)`). One that fails leaves the queue alone, so the next opening skill and then the head's first plan run. The failure is written to `head.jsonl` as `opening_failed`; it does not set the plan's last result, the epoch or a `skill_failed` event. A failing skill of the head's own plan behaves as before. The camera is levelled whether or not the view could be set. `--no-view` leaves out the view; `--no-level`, `--no-objects` and `--no-hud-flow` leave it out as well, since without detections or the HUD it has nothing to read.

What was not run: the touchpad pulse on the console, the time the camera takes to settle after a press (more than a second in the recordings, 1.5 s is a margin), a quad rider's box in more places than the sample, and anything in a vehicle. A start in the game's idle camera, which shows the figure off the middle and from the side, reads as no figure; the first press ends it and the round goes on from there. A detector that fails on every frame looks like a hidden figure and costs four presses, which return the view to where it began, and a failed skill. The survey was not run on the console; the round on pavement, at night and after a respawn is only as good as the detector's boxes there, and what the recordings could not show is how the camera behaves after a press in a view the pilot has never been in (the pavement sessions pressed nothing). The recordings `20261112-111357`, `20261112-101320` and `20261111-183820` were trimmed after the sample was taken, so `evaluate.py --detect F` can re-read only 86 of the 90 frames, four of them are other frames now, and 82 give the stored detections to a pixel.

For the views in a vehicle `read_view` takes bands per mode and reads unknown without them. To record a round for them, on an ordinary game screen with the human driver seated in a safely parked car: `ps5rp record start -o /private/tmp/p56-vehicle-views --note "P56 car: two rounds of four camera views"`, one short physical touchpad press per view, two seconds between them, two complete rounds, then `ps5rp record stop`; the same for a motorcycle. The pad logs hold only some touchpad presses, so write each press time, the frames before and after and the view you see into a small sidecar file. The car body and the rider need their own bands, and both need a first-person picture with no body.
