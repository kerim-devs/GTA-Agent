# P56: the view the pilot needs

The pilot now reads which of the four views the camera is in from the size of the figure's box, and a session begins by setting the far view before it levels the camera. The reading is `read_view` in `pilot/src/pilot/camera.py`, the skill is `set_far_view` in the same file, the opening is `opening()` in `pilot/src/pilot/cli.py`. There was no console, daemon, `ps5rp` command or recording write; the recordings were read, and the detector was run natively on frames of them and of `lab/camera/views/`.

The object reader now hands the conductor the person it cut out as the figure and the vehicle it cut out beside it (`split_player`, `Detector.detect_split`, `Reader.figure`, `Reader.figure_vehicle` in `objects.py`; `Observation.figure` and `figure_vehicle` in `contract.py`; delivered in `run_play`). They carry the capture time of the detections they come from, and the filtered list the avoider and the entry skill use is unchanged. The reading takes the figure's height and where its feet stand, as fractions of the frame: far 0.23 to 0.345 high with feet at 0.66 to 0.86, middle 0.38 to 0.475 and 0.82 to 0.96, near 0.53 to 0.65 and 0.965 or lower. The gaps are left unknown on purpose. A figure that is a rider (a vehicle under its feet and across its body; a motorcycle always, a car or truck only when not wider than 2.2 times the figure), that shares its place with a second person, that stands off the middle of the frame or that the detector is unsure of (under 0.8) is not read. A view is named only once the readings of half a second, from three detections, agree and none of the last second says another.

On the 90 frames from ten of Kerim's recordings (`lab/camera/sample/`, labelled by eye, with the contact sheets and the raw detections) and the eight pictures, 50 frames are far, middle or near. 46 are named right and none wrong. The other four are one frame where the figure sprints past a truck (0.375 high, in the gap), two where an onlooker or a larger person at the edge stands in the figure's place, and the picture in which a quad hides the figure. With the bands fitted on the other recordings and read on the one left out, 44 of 50 are named right and none wrong. All 18 first-person frames read as no figure, and the four motorcycle frames are not read. The bands were fitted on this sample and tested on it, so the leave-one-recording-out figure is the honest one, and the sample is Sandy Shores and Los Santos by day and at night. `lab/camera/evaluate.py` prints the table and refits.

The skill is a closed loop on that reading, because four presses come back to where they began. Far and steady: done, with the view and the presses. A named view that is not far, or no figure for 1.5 s (first person and a hidden figure look the same): a pulse of the touchpad of 0.1 s, then 1.5 s of settling, then a reading again; if no readable figure shows by then, one step forward of 0.4 s swings the camera behind it. At most five presses and a hard limit of 30 s (8 to 40 as an argument); four presses that showed no figure at all end it as failed. It presses only on the game's screen, only after the mode has read foot for a full second, never with a rider's vehicle under the figure and never on a detection older than 0.6 s; every end is a neutral pad. It cannot be cut short, so the head's first plan waits behind it as it waits behind `level_camera`.

The runner takes the opening skills as a group (`SkillRunner.replace(..., opening=True)`). One that fails leaves the queue alone, so the next opening skill and then the head's first plan run. The failure is written to `head.jsonl` as `opening_failed`; it does not set the plan's last result, the epoch or a `skill_failed` event. A failing skill of the head's own plan behaves as before. The camera is levelled whether or not the view could be set. `--no-view` leaves out the view; `--no-level`, `--no-objects` and `--no-hud-flow` leave it out as well, since without detections or the HUD it has nothing to read.

What was not run: the touchpad pulse on the console, the time the camera takes to settle after a press (more than a second in the recordings, 1.5 s is a margin), a quad rider's box in more places than the sample, and anything in a vehicle. A detector that fails on every frame looks like a hidden figure and costs four presses, which return the view to where it began, and a failed skill. The recordings `20261112-111357`, `20261112-101320` and `20261111-183820` were trimmed after the sample was taken, so `evaluate.py --detect F` can re-read only 86 of the 90 frames, four of them are other frames now, and 82 give the stored detections to a pixel.

For the views in a vehicle `read_view` takes bands per mode and reads unknown without them. To record a round for them, on an ordinary game screen with the human driver seated in a safely parked car: `ps5rp record start -o /private/tmp/p56-vehicle-views --note "P56 car: two rounds of four camera views"`, one short physical touchpad press per view, two seconds between them, two complete rounds, then `ps5rp record stop`; the same for a motorcycle. The pad logs hold only some touchpad presses, so write each press time, the frames before and after and the view you see into a small sidecar file. The car body and the rider need their own bands, and both need a first-person picture with no body.
