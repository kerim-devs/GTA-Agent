P24 adds `pilot calibrate`, a recorded measurement session using the same `ControllerRuntime`, interrupt handlers and cleanup function as `run_play`. It runs delay, camera, walk, press, map, wheel, phone, optional car, detector and head checks in that order. Repeated or comma-separated `--only` and `--skip` flags preserve that order. `--car` is required for the vehicle check. `--yes` bypasses the German Enter prompt; `--shadow` performs the readers and passive diagnostics without any controller writes. Each session writes `calibration.json` beside its recording, including check status, reason, numbers, timestamped frame observations, command results and the numeric series behind the measurements. `--head off` explicitly skips the head request.

The implementation uses only Options, Circle, Cross, L1, D-pad Up, Triangle and the two sticks. Cross is held once on a freshly read pause screen and is released as soon as the map is observed. A failed precondition sends no controller state, including no exit neutral for an entirely passive session. Active checks release the pad on success and failure. An unexpected pause, map or phone gets the necessary Circle back-out; other screens get no guessed button. A controller conflict stops the session, records the reason and skips the remaining checks. The 240-second deadline prevents further active input. The detector runs alongside the reading loop; the one head response is timed and recorded, never executed as a skill. Diagnostic shutdown is bounded so a stuck inference cannot indefinitely delay cleanup; unfinished cleanup is recorded in `cleanup_pending`.

The independent in-process console in `pilot/tools/sim_console.py` applies delayed pad states, a camera dead zone and gain, walking, minimum button durations, reversible map motion and timed vehicle transitions. The HUD and minimap readers, image motion and `waypoint.map_shift` all process actual recording images. Pause, map, phone and the initial game use existing HUD fixtures. The wheel uses frame 10940 at source time 538.037 seconds from `recordings/20261110-093238`; the fixture receipt is in `pilot/tests/fixtures/calibrate/README.md`. The tilted view copies a real tilted minimap onto the game image. Moving texture preserves the reader's fixed borders, arrow, compass, map scale and crosshair. The simulator never connects a live Bridge.

## Review

Checked in the night of 10 to 11 November, on the simulated console only; nothing was sent to the real one, and the first real run is Kerim's. The command is what the card asked for, and its limits hold in the code as well as in the tests: the buttons it can send are the six named ones and the sticks, Cross goes out in one place and only on a pause screen that was just read, every check that sent something ends with a neutral pad, and a run that sent nothing does not even write the neutral one. Six things were changed here.

A late frame. One tick without a fresh picture ended the check. The simulated console never drops a frame, the real one does: three times in each of the two eight-minute sessions of 10 November, over the cable. A run of one to two minutes would have lost a check about every second time. A check now waits out up to half a second without a picture, as play does, and the runtime lets go of the pad by its own rule in between.

A menu that shows up late. When the phone or the pause screen did not appear within the wait, the check was given up while the picture still showed the game, nothing was closed, and the menu that arrived a moment later stayed open and every later check was skipped. The back-out now waits for the picture to settle before it looks, and looks again when the picture changes or goes away under its press, four times at most.

The wait. Every check waited 1.2 s for its answer, which is less than the 0.6 to 1.1 s of the Wi-Fi recordings leaves room for. The delay check comes first, and the checks after it wait twice the slowest delay it saw plus 0.4 s, and never less than 1.2 s.

The car. A vehicle map counted after half a second. On foot the minimap reader held a false one for up to 0.83 s in session 20261110-231512, so it is a second here, as in `vehicle_entry.py` since the same night. The table above still says half a second; `lab/calibrate/measured.json` is from after the change, and the sentence about keeping `CONFIRM_SECONDS = 0.5` is out of date.

The detector. Missing weights raised out of the check and ended the session before the head was asked. They end the detector check now.

Where it records. Sessions went into `lab/calibrate/` inside the repository. They go to `~/recordings/calibrate/` now, next to the play sessions.

Tests were added for each of these, and for two cases the simulated console did not have: a link faster than the press, where the map is in the picture while Cross would still be held, and an open phone on foot, where the minimap behind it reads as ordinary play. Eleven deliberate breaks were tried against the tests: a late frame ending the check, a picture that stays away never ending it, the back-out not waiting, the back-out giving up, the fixed wait, half a second for the car, the weights, Cross held on after the map is there, a check started from a menu, no neutral after an abort, and the runtime's shadow flag turned off. Ten were caught; the shadow flag was not, and it changes nothing, because a shadow run never reaches a send. The default gate passes with 978 tests in 60 s. The two simulated sessions take 51 and 86 s.

What the simulated console cannot say: whether the phase correlation that measures the camera holds up on a real street with traffic in view, whether the HUD reader names the weapon wheel and the phone on the live picture as reliably as on the recording, and how long the walk really carries the character at full stick. The head number is one cold request through the Codex command line, which is slower than the server the stream uses; it is an upper bound and not the figure for `Conductor.head_latency`.
