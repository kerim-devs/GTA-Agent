Before this change, distance alone needs three coherent captures spanning at least 0.5 seconds and at most the 3-second window. Speed needs at least four samples, a one-second span, three inliers and at least 55% agreement, then a fit to the inliers. At the ideal 0.25-second cadence the span condition actually takes five captures. An established fit quarantines a contradictory distance until at least five coherent pending captures span one second. Neither an isolated outlier nor a late duplicate renews freshness. Speed expires 1.5 seconds after the last accepted capture, not after OCR completion.

The new early path requires metre quantisation, at least four captures spanning 0.75 seconds, all samples inside consensus, and residuals within one printed metre. Coarse kilometre values and disputed early samples still need the full second. At an assumed 150ms OCR completion time, first speed appears at 0.90 rather than 1.15 seconds after the first capture, with up to one sampling interval and one caller tick added by phase. Synthetic standstill accelerations of 2, 4 and 8m/s² confirm those times; kilometre startup remains 1.15 seconds and can initially report zero because the print has not changed by 10m. A slope is still a lagging speed estimate, not instantaneous ground truth. The early path's capture-time error in those metre ramps is 0.3, 1.8 and 3.2m/s, compared with 0.8, 2.0 and 4.0m/s at the old first output. The existing 1.5-second expiry was retained. More frequent correct OCR renews speed; extending a stale speed clock would be a different, unproven assumption.
