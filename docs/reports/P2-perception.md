## Review

Checked on 10 November. The two label changes are reverted: with the contrast stretched, crops 1437 and 1447 show a washed-out vehicle map whose streets narrow and converge towards the top, so "tilted" was right and the reader gets both wrong. Against the original labels the packaged reader and Trace score 95.8% map accuracy on dev and 93.8% on held, Rings 93.3% and 87.5%, Model 97.5% and 91.7%. The tables above still show the figures after the relabelling and are too high by one crop per split. Everything else was reproduced from the saved harness: routes 34/0/0 and 11/0/0, near bearing within 45 degrees on all of them, arrow median 2.2 and 1.9 degrees, 1.4 ms per reading, and the motion calibration with 8 of 12, 4 of 4 and 10 of 10 accepted pairs. `measurements.json` was regenerated with the original labels.

The tests were checked by breaking the reader. The old fringe rule, a missing confidence cap and a tenfold motion threshold each fail a test. An arrow that always reads zero passed all of them, because the only arrow expectation was a crop with the arrow pointing up. Three fixtures with the arrow pointing back, left and right close that gap.

Two things are worth knowing before the first drive. The reader flags 801 of the 2768 recorded crops as damaged, and since damage now caps the confidence below the driver's threshold, the driver coasts on those frames. That is right for the Wi-Fi recordings they come from, but the rate on the wired link has to be measured, and three of 38 clean held-out crops are flagged by mistake. The second is that the map class on washed-out frames is unreliable, which costs the driver one idle tick at worst.
