P23 is implemented locally, but its simulation acceptance target is not met. The standalone skill, pixel-box behavior tests, simulator and evidence are present. Standing targets succeed in all 30 nominal-FOV runs, while walking targets succeed in 15 of 30 nominal-FOV runs. Neither case meets the required 8/10 across all FOV and turn-rate combinations. Do not register or rely on this as a validated fighting skill until reach and tracking are calibrated. No console, daemon, controller, PS5_HOST or configuration access was used. `pilot/src/pilot/punch.py` implements the skill interface with duration clamped to 0.1–20 seconds, default 15, and integer hits clamped to 1–6, default 3. Unknown keys, nonnumeric values, booleans, nonfinite numbers and fractional hit counts raise ValueError. Pixel boxes are checked against 1280x720 before normalization. Initial target selection and subsequent proximity/size association follow P16's approach without modifying P16. Ground-shaped candidates can remain associated after a large height reduction. The skill walks and turns toward its target, releases movement before holding L2 and pulsing Circle, resumes pursuit when reach is lost, and never sets R2 or another button. It counts attempts, not confirmed game damage.

Estimated constants are a 0.62-frame reach height, 0.09 horizontal centering tolerance, 0.12-second Circle pulse, 0.65-second pulse interval, maximum right-stick magnitude 0.35, forward stick 0.65, and 0.45 seconds of advancing ground-box captures with pixel width greater than 1.6 times pixel height. The reach height was selected from the offline nominal-camera sweep, where it delivered all requested hits to 30/30 standing targets and 15/30 walking targets; it is not a console measurement. Ground completion can occur before the first attack. The low-health threshold is strictly below 0.3 for 0.5 seconds. Missing health resets that continuous-low interval rather than inventing damage.

Missing, stale, future and regressed detector timestamps immediately release all controls. Detector freshness is limited to 0.5 seconds; continuously unavailable data fails after one second. Fresh empty detections fail initial search after three seconds, and an established missing target fails after one second. Corrupt or tilted minimaps, non-game HUD screens, invalid observations, stop and the overall duration limit produce neutral terminal results. Every terminal result is latched. A repeated capture cannot alone confirm a person staying on the ground.

The defensive player filter rejects a large, low, central, broad person box on initial acquisition. Its estimates are center x at 30–65% of the frame, bottom at least 94%, height at least 48%, and pixel width/height at least 0.55. An established NPC may grow into that region only with center displacement at most 0.12, height ratio 0.8–1.25 and width ratio 0.8–1.35. This prevents the filter from dropping an approaching NPC solely because of clipping while rejecting the supplied leaked-player shape and a leak replacing a missing side target. It cannot establish identity when two boxes become geometrically indistinguishable. That remains an explicit limitation of box-only tracking.

Each of six cases uses ten seeds at every combination of FOV scale 0.5/1/2 and turn-rate scale 0.5/1/2. Every run ended within 15 seconds with a reason and a neutral pad that stayed neutral on another step.

The simulation uses a pinhole camera with pixel boxes, estimated base FOV 70 degrees, turn rate 120 degrees per second, camera offset 0.8 m behind the player and height 1.5 m, person dimensions 0.5 by 1.7 m, and full-stick walking speed 3 m/s. Walking people move laterally at 1.4 m/s; a runaway moves directly away at 4 m/s after its first independently valid hit. Each controller decision sees 0.14 seconds of command-to-picture latency plus a uniformly sampled 0.04–0.16-second detector delay. Detection runs every 0.1 seconds with 0.006 normalized-coordinate jitter, 12% misses and 4% false-box probability. A pulse only counts as delivered when a person is within a fixed 1.6 m world distance and a 25-degree facing cone. These assumptions are centralized in the simulator and do not read the skill's reach threshold. Three hits must land on the same person for a successful run; an internally completed attempt sequence can therefore still fail the experiment.

`lab/punch/height-sweep.json` records an additional 1,800 standing/walking runs at reach heights 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.55, 0.6, 0.62, 0.65, 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9, with the same ten seeds and nine camera combinations. It was generated by loading `tools/sim_punch.py` with `runpy.run_path`, setting `Punch.REACH_HEIGHT` for each value, and calling `run(case, seed, fov, turn)` over that product. No tested height passed the full acceptance matrix. Raising the height improves some narrow-FOV runs while losing wide-FOV runs. A fixed image height encodes apparent size rather than physical reach: FOV 35 and 140 degrees change focal length by about 8.7 times, and clipping further changes the height relation. The evidence supports a calibration blocker for this implementation; it does not prove that every possible controller using temporal box observations is impossible. Resolving this needs measured camera/reach behavior or a separately agreed temporal depth/lock-on strategy, rather than redefining simulated reach to make the result green.

The head sentence is: "punch(duration=15, hits=3): approach a visible person and attempt unarmed L2/Circle punches; movement pauses while attacking. Ends after the requested attempts or a sustained downed box, and fails on lost vision, danger or timeout. Reach is uncalibrated; do not register for live use yet."

Console validation must begin with a stationary, unarmed character and controlled cancellation: confirm L2 lock-on, Circle behavior, neutral release and that the held item/state cannot turn this into a different action. Next measure actual strike reach against pixel height at the available camera distances and FOV, including clipping, before choosing a usable reach rule. Then measure camera turn response, command-to-picture delay, centering tolerance and reversal overshoot. After that measure walk-stick speed and pursuit of a 1.4 m/s target, followed by L2 retention when it moves away. Next establish Circle pulse width, minimum interval, missed attempts and any lock-on lunge. Then label standing, reacting and downed boxes to test the ground criterion and its persistence. Finally measure detector delivery age, consecutive misses, person identity through crossings and the player's leaked-box shapes, and replay low-health, vehicle, pause and damaged-minimap interruptions. None of these console measurements or real damage/knockdown outcomes has been verified here.

## Review

Checked in the night of 10 to 11 November.

The worker stopped where the evidence stopped and said so, which is the right report for this card. What fails is the card's own test more than the skill: it asked for the field of view to be halved and doubled, and a reach that is read off the height of a box cannot be right at three focal lengths at once. The game has one. At the assumed camera, standing people get their hits in 30 of 30 runs and walking people in 15 of 30, most of the misses with the camera turning at half speed. The height at which a punch reaches is one number, and it has to come from the console. The simulated game also knows nothing of the step the character takes towards a locked target, so its reach of 1.6 m is harder than the game's.

One thing would have ended the skill within seconds on the console. It failed on the first minimap reading that is marked damaged, and that mark comes for a tick or two about once a second on a clean cable picture: 276 and 474 of some 14 000 frames in the two sessions of 10 November, in 167 and 222 short runs, 16 ticks at the longest. A damaged minimap, a tilted one and a screen that is not the game now release everything at once and fail only when they last a second. The same measurement goes to the waypoint skill and the conductor's carry-on, which had the same weakness; that is a change of its own.

Two small things. The skill had no sentence for the head as an attribute, only in the report; it has one now, and `stop` has a default reason like the other skills.

Twenty-two breaks were tried and nineteen failed a test. Three are pinned now: attacking a person who is close but off to the side, walking forward while turning to such a person, and taking a vehicle for a person.

It is registered, against the report's advice, because it is bounded, ends neutral, never touches R2, and a punch that misses costs nothing on a stream. The head's sentence says the reach is not tuned.
