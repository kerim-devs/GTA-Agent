# P43: What went wrong in Kerim's recordings

Every recording Kerim makes now gets a list of what went wrong in it: each death, arrest, failed mission and the other incidents below, with its second on the clock of `frames.jsonl`, its frame, a class, a cause where one can be told, the mission and step it belongs to, and the evidence. `pilot/src/pilot/incidents.py` holds the classes and the rules, `pilot/tools/incidents.py <recording>... --out DIR` writes `DIR/<name>/incidents.jsonl`, and `pilot/tools/incidents.py --summary DIR` writes `DIR/incidents.md` over every recording in `DIR`. The incidents of each step are added to the steps of `missions.json` in a field `incidents`; nothing else in that file changes, which I checked for all eleven files against the committed versions. `lab/notes/process-new.sh` runs the whole chain over every finished recording that has none yet.

Over the eleven recordings of 11 November, 160 minutes of which 114 are play on foot or in a vehicle, the tool finds 7 deaths, no arrest, 4 failed and 8 passed missions, 4 retries, 10 wanted episodes, 20 collisions, 14 changes of vehicle, 23 standstills, 6 long reverses and 7 long turns. Deaths, failures and retries are what the tool says on every sheet I looked at. Collisions are not: 6 of the 20 are collisions.

## How the recordings are read

The texts, raw readings and sections that `texts.py`, `mission_notes.py` and P38 wrote are used as they are. What they do not hold is read once more from the video: the tool decodes each recording in one sequential pass and samples it at ten frames a second by the times in `frames.jsonl`, and for each sample keeps the HUD reader's screen, wanted level and health, the minimap reader's kind of map, route and damage flag, the number of red blips, the brightness and the change of the whole picture since the previous sample, how far the map moved under the player arrow, the distance the game prints on the vehicle map (Vision, twice a second while the map is tilted) and a colour signature of the vehicle under the chase camera. These rows go to `DIR/<name>/hud.jsonl`, 1.6 MB a recording, and are reused on the next run; no frames are kept. The pass took 39 to 52 seconds for a recording of fifteen minutes, so the eleven took about seven minutes in one process. The object detector is not run.

How far the map moved is a measurement of my own in `tools/incidents.py` (`map_shift`), not `motion.map_motion`. The minimap turns about the arrow with the camera, so `map_motion`, which takes the displacement of every feature, reads a car that turns in a car park as fast as one on a highway: 10 to 18 pixels per 0.2 s at 409 to 411 s in `20261111-164039`, where the contact sheet shows the car crawling between parking bays. `map_shift` fits a rotation, scale and shift to the tracked map features and keeps only how far the arrow's own point moved. Over Wi-Fi a single pair still means little, because a repeated frame reads 0 and a lost track reads several hundred, so every use takes the median of two seconds.

Kerim's controller leads the picture. The pad's times are the Mac's and the frames' are their arrival, and `docs/tasks/README.md` measured the lead at 0.6 to 1.1 s on the recording of 10 November; the tool shifts the pad by 0.8 s. A recording whose `pad.jsonl` has a single line is read from the picture alone, and the classes that need the hand (standstill, reverse, turn, the retry button) then find nothing.

## The classes

The classes are closed, and every cause belongs to one class. `death` has police, crash, explosion, fight, fall, drowning and unknown. `arrest` has police. `mission_failed` has player_died, player_arrested, target_escaped, vehicle_destroyed, ally_died, seen, left_area, time_out, other and unknown. `mission_passed` has passed. `retry` has checkpoint, mission_start and unknown. `wanted` has lost, death, arrest, mission_end and recording_end. `collision` has carried_on, reversed, left_car, car_dead and unknown. `car_change` has after_crash, mission and other. `standstill` has stuck_vehicle, stuck_foot, fight, camera and waiting. `reverse` and `turn` have one cause each. Every incident also carries `sure`, high, medium or low, from the checks below.

A death is found in two ways. Outside a mission the game shows "AUSSER GEFECHT" on a desaturated picture, which is the HUD reader's `wasted` screen; four deaths in `20261111-182316` and `20261111-183820` were found by it. Inside a mission the game skips that screen and shows "Mission fehlgeschlagen" with "Franklin ist tot." under it, so a failure whose reason names Franklin, Michael or Trevor is also a death. The moment of the death is the last sample with a health bar before the screen. Its cause comes from the fifteen seconds before: stars on ten samples or more make it police; a collision in the ten seconds before, crash; a jump in brightness of more than 60, explosion; red blips on ten samples, health lost in three steps or more, or aim and fire on foot, fight; a vehicle under him with none of these, crash; a health bar over half three seconds before on foot, fall. Drowning leaves nothing these readers see and is never given; such a death reads unknown or fall. The detection is sure, the cause less so: a fall and being run over look the same to these readers.

An arrest is found only by the text recognition, as a line with "verhaftet", "festgenommen" or "busted" anywhere in the frame. The HUD reader has no template for the arrest screen, so an arrest whose text is misread is missed. There was none in the eleven recordings and none on the two overview sheets, so this class is untested.

A failed mission is a banner line that `notes.ending` calls failed, joined with the HUD reader's `failed` screen. The reason is the line under the banner: readings whose middle lies between y 355 and 420 of the frame and within 160 pixels of the centre, outside the banner region, joined top to bottom when the game wraps it, and the best of the readings chosen with `notes.best_reading`. It is kept verbatim and classed by its words: "tot", "gestorben" or "getötet" with the player's name is player_died and with another name ally_died; "verhaftet" player_arrested; "zerstört" or "fahruntüchtig" vehicle_destroyed; "entkommen", "entwischt", "geflohen", "abgehängt" or "verloren" target_escaped; "entdeckt", "gesehen" or "bemerkt" seen; "verlassen", "zurückgelassen" or "zu weit" left_area; "Zeit", "zu spät" or "abgelaufen" time_out; anything else read is other, and nothing read is unknown. The incident's time is the first reading of the reason or of the banner, whichever is earlier, because over a fading scene the reason is often read before the banner. A failure also says what Kerim did next: cross on the footer within fifteen seconds is retry, circle is quit, and without a press, play coming back from a black screen within fifteen seconds counts as retry.

A passed mission is a "bestanden" banner, joined with the HUD reader's `passed` screen; the HUD reader found the one in `20261111-171046` at 825 s that the text recognition did not read.

A retry is a failure with the retry chosen and play back, at the first sample with a health bar after the black screen. Its cause compares the first objective read in the forty seconds after with the objectives of the failed try: a later step of it is checkpoint, its first step is mission_start.

A wanted episode starts at the first star that three readings within a second agree on and ends at the first zero read for two seconds without a star; the menus and cutscenes in between, where the reader says nothing, do not end it. It ends by death or arrest when one falls inside it, by mission_end when a banner falls inside it or a cutscene or loading screen lies between the last star and the zero, and otherwise by lost. After a death the stars stay on screen through the death animation, so stars are ignored until a zero is read or play comes back from a black screen. The level is the highest that three readings within a second agree on.

A collision is a drive whose median map travel over 2.5 to 0.3 s before a moment is 12 pixels a second or more and over 0.3 to 2.3 s after is 5 or less and a quarter of before, with the minimap tilted and the game on screen throughout, the throttle held at least 40 % and the brake at most 20 % of the two seconds before. A stop with the throttle off at that moment that is followed by leaving the vehicle is a parking and is dropped. What followed in the next ten seconds is the cause: on foot after the pedals were held half the time without the map moving is car_dead, on foot otherwise left_car, the brake held while the map moves reversed, the travel back to half of before carried_on, and nothing of these unknown. The drop has to be a stop or nearly one, so a crash that only slows the car is not found by design.

A change of vehicle is a drive section, at least two seconds on foot, and another drive section within three minutes without a cutscene or loading in between, where the colour signature of the vehicle under the camera differs by an L1 distance of 0.4 or more between the last seconds of the first drive and the first seconds of the second. Its cause is after_crash when a collision ending in left_car or car_dead lies in the fifteen seconds before, mission when an objective "Steig in ...", "Hol ..." or "Nimm ..." was read around it, and other. Where the colours cannot be read on both sides it is not counted. The vehicle itself is never seen, so this class is a guess and marked low.

A standstill is ten seconds or more in a foot or drive section on the game screen where every sample's map travel is 3 or less and the median is 1.5 or less, with the hand on the controller at least half the time. Its cause is stuck_vehicle with the pedals held 30 % of the time in a vehicle, fight with aim or fire 20 % on foot, stuck_foot with the stick pushed 30 % on foot, camera with the right stick 30 %, and waiting otherwise. On foot, walking in small steps moves the map little, so a figure that shuffles on one spot counts as standing.

A long reverse is the brake held without the throttle in a drive section for three seconds or more while the map moves; a long turn is the steering stick at full lock for four seconds or more while the map moves.

## The check by eye

For every death, arrest and failure the tool found, and for twenty collisions drawn with seed 43 from all of them, `lab/notes/incident_sheets.py` made a contact sheet of one frame every half second from five seconds before to three after, and I looked at each. `lab/notes/incidents-check/select.py` makes the draw, and the files beside it list what was drawn; the sheets are rebuilt from them and are not committed. After the last change to the rules there were exactly 20 collisions, so the draw is all of them.

All 7 deaths are deaths. The 5 police deaths are shootouts with the police with stars on screen, four of them ending in "AUSSER GEFECHT". Of the two fight deaths, the one at 829 s in `20261111-190825` is Franklin shot from a pursuing car while he drives, which a first version called crash because he sat in a vehicle; the rule now asks for red blips or hits before it calls a vehicle a crash. The one at 678 s in `20261111-164039` is Franklin run over by a car during the gunfight with the Vagos, so fight is the situation and a car the cause. All 4 failures are failures with their reason read correctly; the one in `20261111-185323` was first dated 123.0 s, five seconds after the banner stood on screen, and is now dated 118.1 s from the reason line. No arrest was found and none was seen.

Of the 20 collisions, 6 are collisions: the tow truck into a traffic light pole at 418 s, the car into a police car and up the barrier at 794 s and the car against a fence at 279 s in `20261111-165543`, the van into a lamp post at 234 s and against a parked car at 191 s in `20261111-171046`, and the car into another that flipped at 331 s in `20261111-190825`. The other 14 are driving on at speed or slow manoeuvring that the map reads as a stop: at 879 s in `20261111-165543` and 126 s in `20261111-182316` the car drives straight at speed. The cause is the measurement. On a straight road the minimap is the same picture shifted along the road, the tracked features are its edges, and their shift along the road cannot be measured, so the map travel falls to 2 to 4 pixels a second while the car does 100 km/h. Stopping at a closed gate, parking with the engine running and turning in a car park make up the rest. Of the 6 true ones, the follow-up is right for 3 (carried_on at 418, 794 and 234 s), wrong for 2 (carried_on at 279 s, where the car stays at the fence; unknown at 191 s, where he drives on) and cannot be told from the sheet for the reversal at 331 s. The sheets also show collisions the tool missed: a spin after a crash at 506 s in `20261111-164039`, a spin at 194 s in `20261111-185323` and the second crash over the barrier at 800 s in `20261111-165543`, which a first version had found. How many it misses overall is not measured. The first version of the collision rule, with one-second windows and means, found 58 collisions, of which about four in ten on ten sheets were collisions; the present one finds fewer and is no more precise, so the class is marked low and its counts should be read as an upper bound with a third of them true.

Going the other way, I made sheets of one frame every two seconds over the whole of `20261111-164039` and `20261111-190825`, 451 frames each, and wrote down every death, failure and arrest I could see before comparing. In `20261111-164039` there is one failure, "Mission fehlgeschlagen" from 682 to 687 s after Franklin goes down at 678 s, and no death screen and no arrest; the tool has the death and the failure. In `20261111-190825` there are two failures, at 84 to 89 s after Franklin dies in the shootout at the police car and at 832 to 836 s after he dies in the pickup, the passed banner at 418 to 422 s, and no death screen and no arrest; the tool has both deaths, both failures and the passed mission. It missed nothing of these classes in the two recordings. At two seconds a frame a banner of four seconds cannot be missed, but a death screen of under two seconds could be.

Wanted episodes, standstills, changes of vehicle, reverses and turns were not checked by eye. The wanted episodes agree with the stars visible on the death sheets and on the overview of `20261111-190825`, where three stars stand from the first frame to the death at 82 s and again after the retry. Standstill, reverse and turn rest on the same map travel as the collisions and inherit its fault on straight roads in the other direction: a car standing still is read correctly, a car moving straight can be read as standing.

## How sure each class is

Death, failed and passed missions and retries are sure: every one found was right on its sheet, and the overview check found nothing missed in two recordings. Their causes are as sure as the line under the banner is readable, which it was every time. The cause of a death is medium. Arrest is untested and depends on the text recognition alone. Wanted is medium: the star reader is the P25 reader, and an episode ends correctly only if a zero is read for two seconds afterwards. Collision is low, with 6 of 20 true, and its follow-up is right for half of those. Change of vehicle is low because no vehicle is ever recognised. Standstill and reverse are medium for vehicles standing still or reversing slowly and low for straight driving; turn is low. None of the classes sees what the object detector would see: whether the obstacle was a car, a pole or a person, or whether the vehicle he climbs into is the one he left.

## Keeping up with new recordings

`lab/notes/process-new.sh [RECORDINGS_ROOT] [OUTPUT_ROOT]` takes `/Users/kerim/recordings/kerim` and `/Users/kerim/recordings/derived/kerim` when no roots are given. For every folder under the first whose `meta.json` has `seconds`, it skips the folder when `OUTPUT_ROOT/<name>/incidents.jsonl` exists; otherwise it runs sections, texts, missions and incidents in that order into `OUTPUT_ROOT/<name>/`, one recording at a time, and then rewrites `OUTPUT_ROOT/summary.md` and `OUTPUT_ROOT/incidents.md`. A folder whose `meta.json` has no `seconds` is reported as still being recorded and left alone. The sections step runs `pilot/tools/sections.py` of this checkout, which this branch does not have; until P38 is merged the script says so for each recording ("no sections.json and no .../pilot/tools/sections.py in this checkout; merge the sections tool (P38) or put sections.json into ...") and goes on, and a `sections.json` placed in the output folder by hand is used instead. I ran it with a scratch recordings root holding a link to `20261111-192328` and an unfinished folder, and a scratch output root: without sections it stopped that recording with the message above and left the unfinished one alone, and with sections in place the whole chain took 19 seconds for the 293 s recording and wrote both summaries. I did not run it on the default roots, since nothing under `/Users/kerim/recordings/` is mine to write.

## Reproducing

From the repository root, `lab/notes/run-incidents.sh` runs `pilot/tools/incidents.py` over the eleven recordings into `lab/notes/out` with the sections in `lab/notes/sections` (the first four copied from the P38 tree) and writes `lab/notes/out/incidents.md`; the first run decodes the videos for `hud.jsonl`, about seven minutes, and later runs take seconds. `python3 lab/notes/incidents-check/select.py` draws the sheets' incidents, and `pilot/.venv/bin/python lab/notes/incident_sheets.py <recording> --incidents lab/notes/incidents-check/<name>.jsonl --out <dir>` and `... --overview 2 --out <dir>` make the sheets.

`pilot/tests/test_incidents.py` pins, on sequences of samples, readings and pad lines, the class of each kind of reason line, a failure naming Franklin becoming a death, a failure and a retry from the checkpoint, circle on the footer being no retry, a sudden stop with the throttle held being a collision and the same stop with the brake not, single repeated frames at speed being no collision, leaving the car after a collision, a wanted episode lasting from the first lasting star to the first lasting zero with one odd reading changing nothing, a single star reading being nothing, stars cleared by a cutscene, a standstill needing the hand, a long reverse against a short one, the pad's lead, the incidents field leaving the rest of `missions.json` alone, and a recording without controller lines.

From `pilot/`, `env -u PS5_HOST .venv/bin/python tools/gate.py --full` ends with "2742 passed, 1 skipped, 236 xfailed in 611.62s" and "gate passed in 611.92s (full)".

## What the next person must know

The collision class needs a speed that does not come from the minimap. The printed distance gives one only while a route is set and was read on about a third of the tries; the judge on the gaming PC, asked whether the vehicle is moving, or the object detector on the frames around a candidate would decide the 14 false ones of this check. Until then the collision counts are an upper bound. The pad lead of 0.8 s is taken from the Wi-Fi recording of 10 November and not measured on these; the rules use windows of a second or more around it, so an error of a few tenths changes little. `scene.py` still labels the pale objectives of these recordings subtitle (see P39), so 22 of the 27 missions in `missions.json` end after a minute without an objective, and the outcome of 16 of the 25 missions played is unknown; banners outside a mission are not lost but listed as outside any mission.
