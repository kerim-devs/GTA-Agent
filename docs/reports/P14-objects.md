P14 is implemented as a measured baseline, but it is not done or ready for P16/P18 to rely on. The final 80-frame evaluation finds 28 of 103 large objects, or 27.18%, against the required 85%. It invents 64 of 98 scored reports, or 65.31%, against the maximum 10%. I could not meet those quality requirements with the tested permissively licensed models. Runtime, player exclusion, bounded memory and the default software gate pass; a passing software gate must not be interpreted as acceptance of the detector.

`pilot/src/pilot/objects.py` provides `Detector`, `Detector.detect(frame)` and `Reader`. Input is a 1280x720 RGB uint8 array; output is a tuple of the existing `contract.Detection` values in full-frame pixels. The six kinds use the existing contract without changes. `Reader.poll(frame, t)` returns a newly completed `(capture_time, detections)` result or `None`, accepts a copied frame only when its single persistent worker is free, and never queues busy frames. Its default admission interval is 0.05 seconds. Errors are exposed through `reader.error` and an empty detection tuple; successful work clears the error. `close()` stops admission and joins the worker, so shutdown waits for an in-flight native inference. No conductor, skill, CLI, prompt or contract wiring was changed.

The model is the official YOLOX Tiny 416x416 ONNX export from [Megvii-BaseDetection/YOLOX release 0.1.1rc0](https://github.com/Megvii-BaseDetection/YOLOX/releases/download/0.1.1rc0/yolox_tiny.onnx). The project and published weights use Apache 2.0; the accompanying license is saved in `pilot/models/LICENSE.yolox`. ONNX Runtime 1.31.1 runs locally with the CPU provider and two inference threads. The 20,219,662-byte weights are ignored by git. The fetcher and detector verify SHA-256 `427cc366d34e27ff7a03e2899b5e3671425c262ea2291f88bb942bc1cc70b0f7`. From `pilot`, `.venv/bin/python tools/fetch_models.py` installs or verifies them; missing or corrupt weights fail with that fetch command. Inference never downloads a model. Runtime telemetry is disabled before constructing the session. Network use was limited to obtaining packages, model weights and their accompanying license/source package; recordings and project data were not uploaded.

The benchmark uses seed 1414, with 24 frames from each clean cable recording and 32 from the long Wi-Fi recording. All 80 raw frames were visually labelled without model predictions, with the player separately marked. Coordinate errors in initial contact-sheet readings were corrected against individual images, and a final class audit consistently labels pickups as trucks. Frames, labels, original capture times, video indices and SHA-256 digests are in `lab/objects/`. The first 26 selected frames have no visible player, and 51 of the full 80 have a labelled player. The long recording really spans about 696 seconds on the capture clock; its MP4's nominal playback duration is 473.8 seconds. `capture_time` comes from `frames.jsonl`, separately from `video_time`.

At same-kind IoU 0.5, the final found/missed/invented counts are person 2/15/11, car 23/51/39, truck 8/36/12, bus 0/14/2, motorcycle 1/1/0 and bicycle 0/0/0. Large-object found/missed counts are person 2/9, car 19/29, truck 7/25, bus 0/11, motorcycle 0/1 and bicycle 0/0. The scoring pool covers vehicles wider than 60 pixels and people taller than 40 pixels; the recall gate uses widths greater than 100 and heights greater than 80. Twenty-eight smaller predictions are explicitly ignored because smaller objects were not exhaustively annotated. Wrong-kind reports and duplicate reports are inventions, not successful localizations. Exact boxes, per-frame misses and inventions are retained in `lab/objects/metrics.json`.

The player filter uses measured camera-relative geometry: ordinary on-foot figures are low and central, shoulder views can fill either edge, and ridden vehicles occupy the lower central area. It reports the player on 0 of 80 frames, below the 2% limit, and removes 47 detections matching labelled players. It also removes one real non-player vehicle in frame 068, or 1.25% of sampled frames: a vacated car is detected as a broad truck box in the central foreground. That collateral removal is recorded rather than hidden. The filter was derived and evaluated on this same sample, so its zero observed leakage is not an independent generalization result. Camera changes, close non-player people and unusually large vehicles remain risks.

From `pilot`, `.venv/bin/python tools/evaluate_objects.py` produced `mean_ms: 17.52`, `p95_ms: 21.55`, `large_recall: 0.27184466019417475` and `invented_fraction: 0.6530612244897959`. Timing includes preprocessing, inference, decoding, suppression and the player filter after warm-up while a separate process keeps one CPU core busy. The 30 Hz caller received 15.0 completed results per second. Poll calls averaged 0.0669 ms, with 0.1526 ms p95 and 0.1582 ms maximum. The measured speed passes the 40 ms and ten-results-per-second requirements. The evaluator records `recall: false`, `precision: false`, `player: true`, `speed: true`; its successful exit means measurement completed, not that all quality gates passed.

`.venv/bin/python tools/evaluate_objects.py --memory-only` performed 3,000 detections after 100 warm-up calls. `lab/objects/memory.json` records peak process RSS rising from 416,137,216 to 423,723,008 bytes, a 7,585,792-byte increase, or 7.24 MiB. It reaches that plateau after the first 500 measured calls and stays exactly constant at every remaining 250-call checkpoint through 3,000. The probe took 74.42 seconds and passed its 16 MiB growth limit. A default test repeats all 3,000 detections in a fresh subprocess. This is process high-water RSS, not a claim that every allocation is immediately returned to the OS.

The required `.venv/bin/python tools/gate.py` passed: `416 passed, 2 skipped, 1376 deselected, 4 xfailed in 73.94s (0:01:13)` and `gate passed in 74.22s (default)`. Ruff and format checks passed. `lab/objects/gate.txt` keeps the output. The real-frame regression fails both when the detector is replaced with an empty result and when player filtering is removed. It checks real traffic, central players and an off-center shoulder view. After the capture-time metadata addition, lint and formatting were checked again and the documented `--sample` command was executed: `80/80 regenerated frames match SHA-256; indices, capture times, frame IDs and benchmark label digest match.` The hand labels were preserved. `git diff --check` is clean.

Exploratory attempts are in `lab/objects/experiments.json`. Confidence thresholds from 0.05 to 0.50 could not approach the quality targets. Historical release source uses normalized RGB, but the downloaded Tiny artifact produced no detections with either normalized RGB or normalized BGR; raw BGR was the best measured input. The chosen grid decoding matches the upstream decode formula. A temporary YOLOX-S 640 comparison improved large recall only to 44.66% and still invented 56.92% of scored reports while taking 136.30 ms on average. Those exploratory values precede the final five pickup-class corrections and are not the final acceptance benchmark. The larger model was rejected and is not a shipped dependency or model. No tested replacement satisfied both accuracy and runtime, and fitting this small evaluation sample would not demonstrate reliable gameplay perception.

Eight annotated sheets are saved under `lab/objects/sheets/labelled-*.jpg`, with truth green, player pink and retained predictions yellow. `labelled-012.jpg` shows traffic and class confusion, `labelled-048.jpg` shows corruption and shoulder views, and `labelled-064.jpg` shows driving and the collateral filter case. Small and distant vehicles, partially occluded enemies, buses and vehicle-kind confusion fail visibly. Severely damaged frames have uncertain manual boundaries and unreliable predictions. There are no night scenes or bicycles in the sample, so neither is verified. A stronger model with validated GTA coverage, followed by an independent labelled evaluation, remains necessary before P16/P18 can trust this output.

Only the card's owned paths were changed. The existing `feat/pilot-objects` branch is left as a working-tree handoff, without a commit, push or pull request, as required by `docs/tasks/README.md`: "Leave your changes in the working tree; the reviewer commits them." No live-console command or daemon request was made. The blocking result is detection quality, not an unresolved integration change or missing contract field.

## Review

Checked in the night of 10 to 11 November. The frame around the detector is sound: the gate passes, the poller does not delay the loop, memory is flat, the labels and the evaluation can be rerun. The detector itself does not do the job, as the report says, and the cause is not the strict scoring. Scored again with every vehicle kind counted as the same thing and an overlap of 0.3, it finds 43 of 92 vehicles wider than 100 pixels, 14 of 33 of those between 200 and 400 pixels wide, and on only 22 of 63 frames the largest vehicle in the picture; a third of its vehicle reports match nothing, and it finds 3 of 11 people. One sheet looks fine, the numbers do not.

Two things were not tried: the model ran on two CPU threads although `onnxruntime` offers the CoreML provider here, and the frame was shrunk to 416 pixels before the model saw it. Card P14b continues from this state with a stronger model on the GPU, the frame at its native resolution, a scoring that treats a vehicle as a vehicle, and a second labelled set that is not looked at during tuning. This branch is committed but not merged.
