One objective is now one line. Readings of an objective whose texts are the same up to recognition errors or truncation, with at most four seconds between the end of one and the start of the next, are merged. Two texts are the same when their normalised forms have a similarity ratio of 0.7 or more, or when the shorter is the cut-off start of the longer. The four seconds come from the recordings: a misread objective drops out of its box for one to two seconds and its fragment is read as a subtitle, while an objective hidden by a cutscene is gone for much longer, so the old rule that a text returning after an observed blank is a new line still holds, with the blank now counted from four seconds. The line carries the best reading, chosen without trusting the most frequent exact text, because over Wi-Fi nearly every sample reads differently. Each reading is supported by the samples of all readings it matches, weighted by how close they are, cut-off readings never win against the longer reading they begin, and ties go to the reading with the most letters in ordinary words, then to the longest.
