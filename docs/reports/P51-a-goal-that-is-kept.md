# P51: a goal that is kept

The free-roam head now keeps one bounded goal across ordinary head requests. Its round takes a vehicle, drives to a far waypoint, gets out to punch a visible person, escapes the police and starts again. With no vehicle in view it first seeks a road. The implementation is local to the card's write set. There was no console, daemon, Remote Play, model or network call, no recording was changed, and no Git state was written. The reviewer owns committing and integration under `docs/tasks/README.md`.

| Goal | Start condition | Existing skills / steps | Observed reach condition | Limit | Successor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vehicle | On foot with a visible car, truck or motorcycle, or after the road goal | `enter_vehicle`; `walk_road` while waiting for a vehicle | Accepted vehicle movement mode after the active entry skill finishes its confirmation | 90 seconds or 3 issued attempts | Drive |
| Drive | Already in a vehicle, or vehicle goal reached | `set_waypoint` for a shop or pastime from the map's legend, then `drive_route`; the point on the map (`target away`, seeded cardinal direction, `distance=far`) only when the legend gives nothing; continue an observed route | Completed `drive_route` with `route-gone`/`arrived`, with a route observed during this goal or by that running skill | 240 seconds or 3 attempts | Fight |
| Fight | Drive reached, or drive gave up while still in a vehicle | `press triangle`, short `wait`, observe foot mode; look / walk to seek a person, then `punch` | Completed punch skill, which reports pulses in reach or a target remaining down | 90 seconds or 4 attempts | Escape |

Initial free-roam selection requires a known movement mode in gameplay, no screen objective or retained mission intent, and no interior red, yellow or blue marker. Existing marker rules remain in charge until then. Once a goal is active, incidental markers and temporarily unreadable movement do not replace its useful running work. A limit includes time waiting for head responses. An issued retry consumes the attempt budget; ordinary `go_on` answers do not. A failed goal is not immediately selected again. If an escape goal expires while stars remain, the existing police rules keep control without immediately starting another escape goal. Missing wanted readings never count as clearing the police. Route disappearance without a previously observed route never counts as arrival. Punch completion proves the existing skill's observed/pulsed outcome, not independently measured damage.

The seed is generated once per head, logged on every goal answer and accepted explicitly by `MissionHead(goal_seed=...)` for replay. It chooses among the four far-waypoint directions, which only the fallback uses, and among the places of the legend. Seed 51 is fixed in the checked-in replay. Goal, step, action and the reason for keeping or changing the plan travel through the validated response contract into `head.jsonl`. The single free-roam handover leaves the existing mission and failure/blip rule blocks intact. Existing recovery plans are reused inside active goals. All 15 frozen cases with an objective retain their previous rule and plan.

`go_on` has an empty plan and never calls `replace` or `append`. It retains the exact active skill and queue, including on `plan_ending`, and preserves epoch rejection of overtaken answers. If a skill finishes while a keep answer is in flight, the conductor requests the next step immediately instead of waiting for the quiet timeout. An expected movement-mode change keeps the entry skill alive until it finishes confirmation, and an unreadable mode is not a replacement reason. A waypoint skill keeps ownership of its own map until it closes it. Stars, health below one third, death/arrest, an objective or the goal deadline stop goal-owned work before the next skill tick, even before the flow handler can return early. A `goal_end` row records that interruption.

The offline command is `pilot/.venv/bin/python lab/goals/replay.py`. It loads the pre-change rules from local commit `b396957`, reads recording logs without modification, runs one old and one new head per session in request order and writes [the full replay](../../lab/goals/p51-replay.json) and [the comparison with every changed case](../../lab/goals/p51-replay.md). ## Review of 12 November

The review found defects that this section records with their repairs. A skill that the conductor itself stopped for the goal (`goal_limit`, `goal_stars` and the like) was met as a failed skill, so each give-up by limit was followed by a goal-less step of the failure rules; the reasons that start with `goal_` are no failure now, in the rules and in the goals. `go_on` was given for any running skill; it now needs a skill that fits the way the figure moves. `drive_route` does not fit a figure on foot, because throttle and wheel mean nothing to it. `walk`, `walk_road`, `walk_to`, `face`, `punch`, `melee` and `fight` do not fit a figure that is seated, because the stick then steers the vehicle and the buttons do other things. `enter_vehicle` crosses from one to the other by design, and `set_waypoint`, `look`, `press`, `wait`, `shoot` and `level_camera` do the same in either; an unreadable mode says nothing against any of them. A `vehicle_exit` or `vehicle_enter` event in the request counts as the latest word on where the figure sits, and a later one outranks an earlier one. The interruption event reached the head twice and now reaches it once. A model behind the rules can no longer hand in a goal: its schema allows `goal: null` only, and the conductor ignores a goal from any head that does not say it owns goals. A `go_on` that finds nothing running, and carries no goal, is refused and answered with a short wait instead of an immediate new request. An answer that the conductor drops as out of date now gives back what it consumed in the goals, so that the next decision on the same failure still finds its recovery and its try. A goal's answer used to replace the opening `level_camera` and anything else that cannot be cut short; it waits behind it now.

The limits the card names are pinned as literal numbers in the tests, the conductor test for `go_on` uses event kinds that occur in the sessions, and a separate test has an entry or an exit end the step.

The drive goal's destination is a place from the map's legend. The first call asks by kind, a shop or a pastime by the seed, and `set_waypoint` reports the place and the distance it set and the places it read on the way. The goal keeps these, follows the waypoint in a step of its own without spending a second try, and names the place and the distance in its sentence and in `head.jsonl`. From then on the pilot asks by name for a place it has not been to; a place counts as been to when its trip ended in arrival or ran out its time, and the memory lives in the object that keeps the goal, across goals. All of them are open again once every known place has been visited. When a place is not in the legend the goal takes another from the list the failure named, and only when that list holds no shop or pastime does it fall back to `target away`. Missions are never chosen unasked, and entries of no known sort (the legend of the town prints `Chinesen`) are not either. An escape from the police and the rules that answer an objective keep `target away` and `target mission`. The rules that route a free roam without a goal (`vehicle_waypoint`, `foot_drive`, `failed_vehicle_skill`) ask the legend too.

The replays above were rerun on this state with the same command. The four sessions give 10, 16, 14 and 23 replacement decisions and 14, 11, 12 and 15 keep decisions; against the first version of this report the first two move by one each, because a drive step that was kept now ends when the judge says the figure is on foot, and a walk that was kept ends when it says the figure is seated. The numbers still count decision opportunities on recorded runner states and prove nothing about what the figure would have done.
