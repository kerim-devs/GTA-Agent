P25 extends the stateless HUD reader for the recorded German 1280x720 layout. It counts the white or dim filled stars with dark outlines in five fixed right-to-left slots. Pale blue silhouettes without those outlines are inactive slots. A partial or inconsistent row abstains, including strong displaced star contrast that cannot safely establish zero. A zero also needs a visible gameplay HUD and an empty corner without white clipping or strong codec block edges. Non-game screens abstain. A readable star row does not require the rest of a damaged Wi-Fi frame to be clean.

The core and outline masks use the intersection of the real rightmost stars in Wi-Fi frames 10900, 11588 and 12530, at RGB rectangle x=1195:1219, y=34:58. During mask calibration, dark pixels below 100 define the outline and pixels above 190 inside the filled external outline define the core. Runtime contrast and coverage thresholds also accept the evidenced dim star phase. The other slots are offset by 19 pixels. The embedded German death template comes from frame 12745 at x=296:984, y=292:412 with stride 4. The reader correlates red chroma with the glyph layout and requires a predominantly desaturated background outside the banner. SceneText can only confirm pixels that already resemble the banner. No templates are loaded from disk at runtime.

labels.json contains 1,071 manually reviewed samples with source recording, decoded ordinal, capture timestamp and RGB SHA-256. There are 351 nearest-frame Wi-Fi samples for targets at 500–650 seconds (350 strictly inside the interval; the first is at 499.9743 seconds) and 720 additional negative-sequence samples: 240 before the wanted sequence and 240 from each clean recording. Duplicate nearest-frame selections are removed. Visible star counts and readability are separate from the reviewed negative-sequence context: stars_present=false includes clipped, damaged and non-game frames on those sequences, while wanted=null explicitly says the single frame cannot establish a count. The annotation is not a detector-generated ground truth. Clear positive samples include the one-frame two-star transition at ordinal 11835, between one-star 11825 and three-star 11850. Four and five stars are unmeasured.

Capture timestamps come from frames.jsonl, normalized by its first t. The Wi-Fi video has 14,214 decoded frames and a nominal 473.8-second MP4 duration, but spans 695.8566 seconds of capture time.
