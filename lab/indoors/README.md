Run `pilot/.venv/bin/python lab/indoors/measure.py` from the repository root to reproduce the image measurement, `pilot/.venv/bin/python lab/indoors/starts.py` for the recorded starts, and `pilot/.venv/bin/python lab/indoors/exits.py` for the scripted exits. These scripts only decode local recordings and run readers and skills. They do not contact the console or a model server.

The colour threshold was fixed using seven frames each from `20261113-001124` outdoors and `20261113-025032` indoors, at 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30 seconds. Neither session contributes to the held-out measurement. The image reading counts radar pixels whose maximum RGB channel exceeds the minimum by more than 25; a share below 0.30 says indoors. Unknown frames count against accuracy. This deliberately small first experiment failed qualification and is only corroborating evidence in the runtime.

The held-out indoor set is `20261113-024118`, seconds 2 through 62 inclusive, labelled by the recorded indoor-started barber run, its play log and visual checks at 30 and 60 seconds. The outdoor set contains the 169 entries of `lab/ahead/frames.json` outside the development session and 31 frames at seconds 66 through 96 of `20261113-025632`. The earlier sessions are the documented outdoor walks and hold-ups before accidental entry; the latter interval is after the observed barber exit. Labels and predictions, including source session and timestamp, are in `heldout.json`. Samples are temporally correlated and cover one interior, not general shop recognition.

The 31 old `shop-*` labels in `lab/ahead/indoors.json` have no recoverable source-session provenance. They are historical context only. The 56 outdoor model answers with exact matches to this holdout are compared separately in `heldout.json`; they were not used to tune this reader. The first development experiment and the independent evaluation must remain separate when trying a future reader.

The exit is visibly bracketed between 60 and 65 seconds in `20261113-025632`. Quarter-second samples through 66.75 remain incorrectly indoors. The one-second outdoor samples first say outdoors at 68 and 69, then incorrectly indoors again at 70. This is a 3 to 8 second first-detection delay and a 4 to 9 second two-sample delay at the measured sampling rate, followed by a regression. It is not reliable transition detection.

`starts.json` records the real-reader replay of three barber-frontage starts. The three PNGs are only the original minimap crops, used by regression tests. The replay proves which input the code would emit; the old recording cannot show the consequence of a different input. The icon rule was developed on these starts, so these are regressions, not an independent icon-recognition benchmark.

`exits.json` is explicitly a simulation. Only its initial arrow readings come from the three indoor recordings. Its room geometry, door, movement and accurate place sensor are scripted. A remembered approach is supplied for that scenario because the old recordings contain no saved entrance. These timings must not be presented as measured console exits or as validation of the failed image reader.

`entry.json`, generated by `measure.py`, replays seven frames of accidental-entry session `20261113-022849` through the minimap and indoor readers. The raw reader incorrectly reports indoors before the doorway, then reports outdoors after the welcome scene removes the map. These results cannot establish an entry-detection delay. The runtime does not use a missing-map reading as evidence of outdoors.
