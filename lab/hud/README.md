P15's pixels, labels and reproduction evidence live here. The reader is limited to the observed German 1280x720 RGB layout and default safe area. It does not invoke OCR or access a console. UI anchors are small grayscale or colour masks embedded in the module so runtime and packaged imports need no fixture files. `anchors.py` records each source ordinal, crop rectangle, channel and stride, and reproduces the constants from `20261110-093238/video.mp4`. These are image comparisons, not a general language reader.

`labels.json` contains 200 distinct decoded frames. The random half uses NumPy's seed 15 with 60 frames from the long Wi-Fi recording and 20 from each clean session (`20261110-230229`, `20261110-231512`). The other 100 are selected from visually located rare-screen spans, including their boundaries and damaged transitions. `prepare.py` reproduces the selection and the unlabelled contact sheets; it never invokes the detector or writes inferred ground truth. Output must be a separate scratch directory when reproducing it. A collision uses the closest unused decoded ordinal. `capture_s` is `frames.jsonl.t - first_t`, not nominal video time; `i` is the exact decoded ordinal. Every labelled frame carries a SHA-256 of its decoded RGB pixels.

The reviewed `review/screens-*.jpg` sheets show every screen label's source; `review/bars-*.png` magnify the bar region. Fractions are visual estimates of the bright prefix relative to the whole health track, rounded to two decimals. An omitted health field was not annotated; null means no defensible value because of absence, codec corruption or icon occlusion. Nulls are not numerical zero and are excluded from numerical health accuracy. Numeric labels with a reader abstention count as misses. `health-extra.json`, `health-extra.png` and `health-extra-screens.jpg` retain 20 additional shootout observations and the low/full bar fixture provenance; they are not included in the balanced 200-frame screen score. No reader outputs were used as ground truth.

A top-level title and tab strip, even with a map preview, is `pause`; the expanded interactive map with the side legend is `map`. Cutscenes and uncertain return-to-game transitions are `game`. `loading` means the evidenced black frames, not every aerial transition. The task's rough weapon-wheel window of 95–110 capture seconds did not show a wheel. Actual wheel evidence is around 14.8–15.3 and 537.8–539.2 seconds; phone evidence is around 153.7–156.7. The failed banner is around 34–39.6, the passed banner around 338.8–341.7, and pause/map around 385.2–392.1. Five initial thumbnail annotations were corrected after full-resolution inspection: 342, 343 and 10961 have the wheel; 888 and 898 are black without a failed banner. The pictures were not altered.

Run from `pilot/` with `.venv/bin/python tools/evaluate_hud.py --recordings /path/to/recordings --clean-recordings /path/to/pilot-recordings --output ../lab/hud/evaluation.json`. The default roots are repository `recordings/` and `~/recordings/pilot`. The evaluator verifies source hashes, decodes outside the timer, warms each frame and times 30 reader calls per frame. It exits nonzero when any numerical acceptance threshold or class coverage fails. `evaluation.json` has the confusion matrix, separate random/rare accuracy, all screen and numerical health misses, and per-call timing. Calibration and evaluation share this corpus and rare frames are correlated; these results do not establish generalisation to other HUD layouts or characters.

The default unit suite uses the lossless full RGB frames in `fixtures/`; it needs neither video files nor console connectivity. `fixtures/manifest.json` preserves their source ordinals and visual expectations. It includes all eight classes, low and full health, a vehicle minimap, an empty blue armour track, full special, black frames and actual compression/icon damage. Additional synthetic corruption tests verify abstention and input safety; they are not counted as real screen-class evidence.

All tracks remain coloured when empty. The reader uses fixed track geometry and brightness relative to nearby translucent pixels, then fits a left-filled prefix. The observed blue tracks are empty. Red damage flashes in the Wi-Fi recording have no reliably visible fill boundary and abstain. A clear red low-health sequence, a measured full-to-empty armour sequence, fully depleted special, wanted stars/flashing, Wasted/Busted, other characters' phones, menu tabs and safe-area variants still need footage. Wanted remains None and death/arrest appearance is not guessed. P13 may call `read(frame, text)` directly; this task does not modify the conductor or promise live-console verification.
